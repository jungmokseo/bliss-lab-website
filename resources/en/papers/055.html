Recent Advances in Anti-inflammatory Strategies for Implantable Biosensors and Medical Implants

Dongwon Lee†, Kijun Park†, Jungmok Seo*

Biochip Journal, 14(1), 48-62 (2020)

DOI: 10.1007/s13206-020-4105-7 ↗

The question behind this study

This review surveys material strategies for reducing inflammation and foreign-body responses around implantable sensors and medical devices. It examines compliant structures, local drug delivery and biomimetic surfaces in relation to cell attachment and fibrous encapsulation.

Responses at implant surfaces

Protein/cell adhesion and encapsulation reduce sensitivity and longevity. The review cautions against transferring material safety between implant applications.

Balancing insertion support and flexibility

Flexible probes require insertion stiffness; biodegradable reinforcement supplies temporary support before restoring compliance. Foreign-body responses can nevertheless persist.

Tissue responses versus device lifetime

The reviewed DNNC–PEG-DA comparison examines macrophages and capsules at 7/30 days. These endpoints do not establish sustained device function.

Different results in cells and in vivo

The review reports reduced fibroblast proliferation and COL1A1 after mTOR knockdown in vitro, without a significant change in capsule thickness in vivo. This account summarizes that review; it does not reanalyze the original data.

Questions about long-term protection

Blood-contact TLP and heated-instrument LIS tests do not establish chronic implant protection; surface durability and in-vivo validation remain unresolved.

Understanding foreign-body responses

Anderson’s review abstract describes macrophage fusion and the influence of surface properties on foreign-body responses. It helps explain the interventions discussed here, without ranking their efficacy.
