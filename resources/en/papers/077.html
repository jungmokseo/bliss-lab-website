Modulus-tunable multifunctional hydrogel ink with nanofillers for 3D-Printed soft electronics

Minkyong Kang, Jae Park, Soo A Kim, Tae Young Kim, Ju Yeon Kim, Dae Woo Kim, Kijun Park*, Jungmok Seo*

Biosensors & Bioelectronics, 255, 116257 (2024)

DOI: 10.1016/j.bios.2024.116257 ↗

Research context and placement

Modulus control, limited swelling and viscoelasticity address tissue mismatch; Fig. 4 and Methods 2.7–2.9 directly evaluate the porcine-skin interface and adhesion. CNT conduction and stretchable sensing form the secondary routing connection.

Research overview and key contributions

THIN uses nanoclay-filled and functionalized-CNT-filled hydrogel variants for tissue-adaptive mechanics and conductivity.

Porcine-skin interface/adhesion tests and customizable stretchable sensors evaluate tissue coupling and electrical function.

Open challenges

Cut recovery and wearable demonstrations do not establish chronic fatigue, dehydration resistance or long-term CNT-exposure effects.

Comparison context

Match filler loading, hydration, strain rate, contacts and gauge geometry when comparing modulus, resistance changes and recovery.

Related external research

3D printing of conducting polymers

DOI: 10.1038/s41467-020-15316-7 ↗

Yuk prints intrinsically conducting PEDOT:PSS structures and neural electrodes; THIN tunes a CNT-filled hydrogel network.

Conduction and hydration differ, so conductivity alone cannot rank them. External printing, properties and device sections were read.
