PUBLICATION 061 / 2021Summary based on selected sections

Rational engineering and applications of functional bioadhesives in biomedical engineering

Jae Park†, Yeonju Kim, Beomsoo Chun, Jungmok Seo*

Biotechnology Journal, 16(12), 2100231 (2021)

DOI: 10.1002/biot.202100231 ↗

The question behind this study

This review examines how tissue bonding and cohesion within a material support the design of functional bioadhesives. It connects antimicrobial activity, cell infiltration, conductivity, self-healing and stimulus response with tissue repair and biosignal sensing.

Adhesion alongside tissue function

A bioadhesive needs both bonding at the tissue interface and cohesion within the material under repeated loading. Water, tissue stiffness and surface architecture, and inflammatory or regenerative conditions all affect its performance. This review connects adhesion design with functions needed for tissue repair and biosignal sensing. Its framework does not assume that one material gives the same adhesive or biological response in every tissue.

Five functions added to adhesion

The contribution is an organized link between five functions—antimicrobial activity, cell infiltration, conductivity, self-healing and stimulus response—and adhesion mechanisms, material choices and applications. Antimicrobial design separates killing from attachment prevention; cell infiltration considers reversible crosslinks, cell-binding sites and pore structure. Conductivity distinguishes ionic from electronic transport, while healing and stimulus response support damage recovery, adhesion switching or controlled release. Table 1 maps this design landscape rather than presenting a new experiment demonstrating uniform superiority of a material.

Different studies, different test conditions

This is a review of multiple studies, without a new common experimental cohort or original-material Methods protocol. Figure 6 presents antifouling and cell-infiltration examples; Figure 7 includes deformation, conduction and cell-related measurements; Figure 8 illustrates rheology, recovered adhesion and appearance; and Figures 10–11 present regeneration and biosignal examples. Comparisons should first align tissue, hydration, loading, adhesion/healing time and assay method, then read bonding, material failure, biological endpoints and signal quality separately. A healing time or animal result from one study is not validation of all functions.

Routes to combining functions

The selected examples show design routes for connecting adhesion with additional functions. Multilayer and Janus structures separate adhesive and antifouling faces, while reversible bonds and pore control provide routes to cell-accessible networks. Conductive composites and polymers address tissue contact and signal transport together, but conductor distribution, flexibility and cellular response remain relevant. The synthesis identifies functional routes and trade-offs; it does not establish that all five functions have been validated together for long durations under one shared condition.

Injured tissue and long-term use

Stronger crosslinking can increase stiffness mismatch, while antimicrobial components, curing residues and degradation products need biocompatibility assessment. Appearance or rheology can recover at a different rate from adhesion or electrical function. The review also identifies the gap between normal-tissue tests and injured tissues with altered pH, inflammatory cells and cytokines. Different conditions and follow-up periods, with supplementary information, raw data and cited originals outside this account’s scope, prevent conclusions about clinical safety or product superiority.

Connection to wet-tissue adhesive tape

The related abstract of “Dry double-sided tape for adhesion of wet tissues and devices” (DOI 10.1038/s41586-019-1710-5) describes removal of interfacial water followed by covalent bonding. This review places such adhesion designs alongside antimicrobial activity, cell infiltration, conductivity, self-healing and stimulus response. The external full text and supplementary information are outside the comparison’s scope; performance comparisons require primary experiments matched for tissue, hydration, loading and timing.
