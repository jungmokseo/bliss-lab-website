Reversible Liquid Adhesion Switching of Superamphiphobic Pd-Decorated Ag Dendrites via Gas-Induced Structural Changes

Dayeong Kim, Jungmok Seo, Sera Shin, Soonil Lee, Kilsoo Lee, Hyunjin Cho, Wooyoung Shim, Han-Bo-Ram Lee, Taeyoon Lee*

Chemistry of Materials, 27 (14), 4964-4971 (2015)

DOI: 10.1021/acs.chemmater.5b01038 ↗

Background

Gas-induced shape changes in palladium-decorated silver dendrites switch the adhesion of water and lower-surface-tension liquids. Excess palladium deposition and oil wetting can eliminate reversibility.

What the study found

Gas-responsive changes in palladium-decorated silver dendrites reversibly switch the adhesion of water and lower-surface-tension liquids.

Switching between air and hydrogen extends control to oily liquids and links changes in surface shape to liquid-contact area.

Figure 5

Questions and limits

The paper identifies an operating limit: excessive palladium deposition leads to oil wetting and loss of reversibility.

How to compare the results

Compare surface tension, liquid volume, palladium coverage, gas composition, and cycle count. Assess adhesion force separately from contact angle.
