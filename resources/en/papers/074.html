Liquid-based electronic materials for bioelectronics: current trends and challenges

Kijun Park, Sangwoo Park, Yejin Jo, Soo A Kim, Tae Young Kim, Sangwon Kim, Jungmok Seo*

Industrial Chemistry & Materials, 2(3), 361-377 (2024)

DOI: 10.1039/d3im00122a ↗

Research context and placement

Conductor sections motivate the primary liquid-routing placement, but this review also covers adhesion, encapsulation and antifouling. Its breadth requires multiple themes and an ambiguity flag.

Boundary case: assigned to the closest interface for navigation. Read the rationale and cross-links together; the primary theme does not delimit the study. Placement of foundational work does not establish direct bioelectronic application.

Research overview and key contributions

This review connects liquid conductors, hydrogels, encapsulation and adhesives as material challenges in bioelectronics.

It broadens material selection beyond conductivity to moisture barriers and interfacial contact.

Open challenges

The conclusion acknowledges continued reliance on solid components for stability and function; all-soft devices remain a perspective.

Comparison context

Tabulated conductivity, strain and cycles come from different materials/tests; compare within operating conditions and device requirements.

Related external research

3D printing of conducting polymers

A PEDOT:PSS device does not validate liquid metals, ionic liquids or encapsulation materials as a class.
