Intelligent In situ Printing of Multimaterial Bioinks for First‐Aid Wound Care Guided by Eye‐In‐Hand Robot Technology

Seol-Ha Jeong, Jihyun Kim, Brendan Craig Thibault, Javier Alejandro Lozano Soto, Fatima Tourk, Joshua Steakelum, Diego Azuela, Violeta Carvalho, Guillermo Quiroga-Ocaña, Weida Zhuang, Mei Li L. Cham-Pérez, Lucia L. Huang, Zhuqing Li, Eleftheria‐Angeliki Valsami, Enya Wang, Nelson Rodrigues, Senhorinha F.C.F. Teixeira, Yuhan Lee, Jungmok Seo, Aristidis Veves, Shabir Hassan, Georgios Theocharidis*, Lance Fiondella*, Su Ryon Shin*

Advanced Materials Technologies, 9(11), 2400060 (2024)

DOI: 10.1002/admt.202400060 ↗

Research context and placement

Direct delivery of bioinks to wounds and support for cell infiltration establish tissue-repair biofabrication. Robot use alone does not establish electronic component integration.

Research overview and key contributions

INSIGHT uses a robot-mounted depth camera to identify wounds at varied locations and angles and print multiple bioinks in situ.

It combines extrusion and spraying for large wounds and uses porous microgels to support cell infiltration.

Open challenges

Abstract-level in vivo claims do not establish emergency-patient use, infection management or safety under arbitrary motion.

Comparison context

Match wound geometry/motion, ink viscosity and cell loading; assess targeting, treatment time and repair separately.

Related external research

In Situ Bioprinting of Autologous Skin Cells Accelerates Wound Healing of Extensive Excisional Full-Thickness Wounds

DOI: 10.1038/s41598-018-38366-w ↗

Albanna describes image-guided layered delivery of autologous fibroblasts and keratinocytes; INSIGHT emphasizes robotic viewpoints and ink/printing-mode versatility.

Cell-delivery reconstruction and microgel-supported infiltration differ therapeutically; healing effects cannot be ranked directly.
