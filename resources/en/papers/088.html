PUBLICATION 088 / 2026Abstract-based introduction

Omniphobic Polymer/Graphene Oxide-Coated PVDF Membrane for Stable Membrane Distillation in Surfactant-Containing Seawater

Jiwon Kim, Kwang-Duck Park, Jungmok Seo, Yun Chul Woo, Dae Woo Kim

ACS Environmental Au, 6(1), 125-133 (2026)

Jungmok Seo, who now leads the BLISS Lab in the School of Electrical and Electronic Engineering at Yonsei University, is a co-author of this paper.

DOI: 10.1021/acsenvironau.5c00199 ↗

The question behind this study

Membrane distillation treats saline water by passing only water vapor across a hydrophobic membrane. When the feed contains surfactants or other components that lower surface tension, the membrane pores can wet and let salt through. This study addresses how to make a wetting-resistant coating with a simple process.

What the study shows

The narrow interlayer spacing of graphene oxide can serve as a vapor pathway, but graphene oxide is intrinsically hydrophilic, so suppressing wetting has required multistep chemical functionalization.

The authors slot-die coated graphene oxide onto commercial PVDF membranes and then dip-coated them in a perfluoropolyether (PFPE) solution in a highly volatile solvent, forming a layer a few hundred nanometres thick. According to the abstract, the membranes maintained more than 99.7% NaCl rejection and more than 15 LMH water flux during long-term direct contact membrane distillation, including with low-surface-tension feeds containing surfactants.

Open questions

The abstract only summarizes long-term operation; the duration, surfactant type and concentration, and the reference membrane conditions need to be checked in the main text. The study concerns water-treatment membranes and does not address biological applications.

Conditions for comparison

Comparisons need matched distillation configuration, feed composition and surface tension, feed and permeate temperatures, operating time and the uncoated PVDF membrane.
