Slippery dopamine–fluoropolymer hybrid surface for improving biliary stent longevity

Tae Young Kim†, Won-Jong Lee†, Yurim Lee, Seo Jung Kim, Sungjin Min, Seyong Chung, Soo A Kim, Keun-Young Yook, Chang-Hwan Moon, Yeontaek Lee, Kijun Park, Dae-Hyun Kim*, Jungmok Seo*

Bioactive Materials, 61, 210 - 228 (2026)

DOI: 10.1016/j.bioactmat.2026.02.003 ↗

Why this research matters

A polydopamine-anchored, fluorinated lubricating interface is designed to reduce sludge and biofilm accumulation on biliary stents. The study evaluates fouling on fragments in mouse gallbladders separately from stent patency in rabbit common bile ducts.

Biliary stent coatings and biofilms

Biliary stents can become obstructed by attached material, biofilms and deposits, alongside tissue responses to the foreign body. ELFS creates a lubricated contact surface to address these problems. Fouling and inflammation can influence each other, but each requires its own evidence.

How the study approaches it

The coating combines polydopamine-based anchoring with a fluorinated lubricating interface to discourage attachment. The aim is to keep the stent surface functioning. This anti-adhesion approach does not imply that the coating kills bacteria or eliminates every foreign-body response.

What was tested

Early immune responses were imaged intradermally in dorsal skin, while a separate subcutaneous test assessed biocompatibility. Fragments in mouse gallbladders were used to study fouling; stents in rabbit common bile ducts were used to study patency, with four rabbits per group. These anatomical settings and endpoints cannot be combined into a clinical patency rate.

What the study found

After six months in mouse gallbladders, ELFS fragments had less bile sludge and biofilm-like accumulation than uncoated fragments. In a separate two-month rabbit common-bile-duct experiment, uncoated stents became blocked while ELFS stents maintained flow. The findings complement one another but differ in site, duration and outcome.

What remains to be answered

Fluorine detected on a retrieved stent could come from the underlying PFP scaffold rather than remaining lubricant. Lubricant retention and sustained surface function therefore need to be distinguished. Correction DOI 10.1016/j.bioactmat.2026.03.015 adds acknowledgements and states that the conclusions are unaffected; it does not revise this study's experimental results.

The six-month mouse gallbladder-fragment study and two-month rabbit bile-duct study use different models. Fluorine signals after two months may arise from the supporting layer and do not establish complete long-term retention of the original lubricant.

How it connects to other research

The related omniphobic coating addresses attachment and thrombosis in blood-contacting devices. ELFS applies a retained-liquid interface to bile ducts, with different fluid and tissue conditions. Vascular thrombosis, biliary deposits and fibrosis are distinct outcomes, so the external abstract cannot establish biliary durability.
