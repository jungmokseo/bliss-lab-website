Slippery dopamine–fluoropolymer hybrid surface for improving biliary stent longevity

Tae Young Kim†, Won-Jong Lee†, Yurim Lee, Seo Jung Kim, Sungjin Min, Seyong Chung, Soo A Kim, Keun-Young Yook, Chang-Hwan Moon, Yeontaek Lee, Kijun Park, Dae-Hyun Kim*, Jungmok Seo*

Bioactive Materials, 61, 210 - 228 (2026)

DOI: 10.1016/j.bioactmat.2026.02.003 ↗

Research context and placement

The lubricated biliary-stent coating targets adhesion, biofilm and immune fibrosis; the neutrophil pathway is an immune connection within the same host theme.

Research overview and key contributions

ELFS uses PDA anchoring and a lubricated interface to address biliary-stent occlusion.

The text separately evaluates early immune responses, fouling of stent fragments in mouse gallbladders and rabbit bile-duct patency.

Open challenges

Six-month mouse gallbladder exposure is not bile-duct patency. Rabbit patency was tested for two months; explant fluorine may reflect PFP rather than retained lubricant. Human clinical outcomes were not demonstrated.

Comparison context

Separate skin immunity, gallbladder fouling and duct-flow models; match dwell time, bile and geometry. Rabbit groups each contained four animals and do not establish long-term clinical lifetime.

Related external research

A bioinspired omniphobic surface coating on medical devices prevents thrombosis and biofouling

DOI: 10.1038/nbt.3020 ↗

Relative to blood-contacting liquid coatings, ELFS examines bile ducts and immune–fibrotic pathways.

Thrombosis, biliary sludge and host fibrosis have distinct conditions and endpoints.
