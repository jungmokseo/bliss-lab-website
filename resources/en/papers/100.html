Bridging IC chips and deformable circuits: A packaging framework for high-density soft electronics

Yurim Lee, Yeontaek Lee, Siyeon Park, Hyein Ham, Hanbit Jang, Yunmin Jung, Sooin Lee, Jungmok Seo*

Materials Science and Engineering R: Reports, 171, 101285 (2026)

DOI: 10.1016/j.mser.2026.101285 ↗

Research context and placement

The read review sections address rigid-IC/deformable-circuit architectures, fine-pitch attachment, isolation and packaging selection. This supports the integration category at the interconnect–chip boundary, without ranking cited experiments.

Research background

Soft electronics must accommodate tissue movement and curvature, while many functions still depend on rigid IC chips. Joining those chips to deformable circuits requires stable electrical connections and a structure that accommodates deformation. The review connects material compliance to system functions and packaging requirements.

Approach and advances

The review distinguishes rigid PCBs enclosed in soft materials, soft electrode front ends coupled to rigid back ends, and stretchable circuits with rigid IC islands. These architectures accommodate deformation in different places. As integration becomes denser, the bottleneck can move from soft components to interconnects and fine-pitch chip attachment, where alignment, electrical contact and isolation must be designed together.

Evaluation and conditions

Packaging should be assessed through adhesion, electrical-junction stability and deformation tolerance. Reported strain values in Tables 2–4 differ in component level and in whether testing is monotonic or cyclic. Maximum strain alone therefore cannot rank joining methods. Resistance changes and failure criteria such as shorts or delamination must be interpreted with their test conditions.

Key findings

The §4.4 framework starts with target-tissue compliance and functional complexity. Chip pitch, pad count, electrical isolation and material compatibility then guide choices such as an interposer or anisotropic bonding. Its contribution is a way to connect requirements to assembly constraints, rather than evidence that one joining method is universally superior.

Limits and open questions

Moisture, sweat and cyclic deformation can act together during long-term use, while stiff chip edges and feedthroughs can challenge protective packaging. Barrier performance must be balanced with compliance and assembly constraints, including sterilization compatibility. The review’s design discussion does not establish clinical safety or long-term durability across all tissues and devices.

Connections to related work

The cited studies help compare which requirements different circuit architectures and chip-joining methods address. A highly deformable interconnect does not by itself establish high-density IC joint yield, isolation or durability, so component and system results must remain distinct. This commentary verifies the review’s classification and framework, without independently auditing the cited originals.
