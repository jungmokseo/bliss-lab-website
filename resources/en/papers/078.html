Postoperative Long-Term Monitoring of Mechanical Characteristics in Reconstructed Soft Tissues Using Biocompatible, Immune-Tolerant, and Wireless Electronic Sutures

Mugeun Lee†, Yeontaek Lee†, Ji Hye Choi†, Hwajoong Kim, Daun Jeong, Kijun Park, Jinho Kim, Jae Park, Woo Young Jang*, Jungmok Seo*, Jaehong Lee*

ACS Nano, 18, 12210-12224 (2024)

DOI: 10.1021/acsnano.4c00396 ↗

Research context and placement

A chip-free wireless system combines a capacitive fiber sensor, inductive coil and suture fixation. It extends component integration to sensor-system assembly without demonstrating an interconnect–chip junction. Antifouling and tissue fixation provide host and tissue cross-links.

Boundary case: assigned to the closest interface for navigation. Read the rationale and cross-links together; the primary theme does not delimit the study. Placement of foundational work does not establish direct bioelectronic application.

Research overview and key contributions

A capacitive fiber sensor, inductive coil and antifouling lubricant form a wireless suturable system. Tendon stiffness is inferred from measured strain through a mechanical model.

The design combines suture-based fixation with chip-free wireless readout. Load-bearing repair sutures and the compliant strain sensor serve different mechanical roles.

Open challenges

The porcine experiment used one animal. Clinical generalizability and rehabilitation benefit remain unestablished; the authors identify surgical sensor removal as a limitation.

Comparison context

Editorial proposal: match joint motion, healing stage, fixation and coil alignment, then validate modeled stiffness against independent mechanical measurements.

Related external research
