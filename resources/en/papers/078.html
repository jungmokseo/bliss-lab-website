Postoperative Long-Term Monitoring of Mechanical Characteristics in Reconstructed Soft Tissues Using Biocompatible, Immune-Tolerant, and Wireless Electronic Sutures

Mugeun Lee†, Yeontaek Lee†, Ji Hye Choi†, Hwajoong Kim, Daun Jeong, Kijun Park, Jinho Kim, Jae Park, Woo Young Jang*, Jungmok Seo*, Jaehong Lee*

ACS Nano, 18(19), 12210-12224 (2024)

DOI: 10.1021/acsnano.4c00396 ↗

Why this research matters

A fiber-shaped capacitive sensor coupled to a coil wirelessly tracks deformation in reconstructed soft tissue. The study follows changing strain signals in one pig with a reconstructed Achilles tendon; it does not directly measure the tissue’s failure strength.

The problem

Following tissue recovery requires repeated measurements during movement. This study uses a suture-like electronic structure to read deformation wirelessly. Measuring movement and mechanically supporting a repair are separate functions.

How the study approaches it

A fiber capacitor is coupled to a coil, allowing deformation to be detected through changes in resonance. A lubricated interface separately addresses attachment from surrounding tissue. The sensing fiber's flexibility does not establish that it can bear the loads of a conventional surgical suture.

What was tested

The in-vivo demonstration used one pig. Movement, fixation and the alignment of the readout coil all affect interpretation. Estimating tissue condition from deformation during movement differs from directly testing the tissue's breaking strength.

What the study found

In the reconstructed Achilles tendon, little strain response appeared during the first two weeks, which the authors attributed to support from surgical sutures. A larger response appeared around week four, then decreased toward week ten under the same leg movement. A multispring model interpreted this as increasing stiffness; it remains a single-animal inference rather than destructive strength testing or a validated clinical diagnosis.

What remains to be answered

Further work needs to establish reproducible fixation and readout, variation between subjects and the relationship between the signal and tissue mechanics. Sensor removal also requires a second operation. The single-animal result does not establish average clinical performance, and unread supplementary model conditions are not inferred here.

How it connects to other research

The related RFID/capacitive suture study addresses deep-wound monitoring with a different placement and measurement target. Its abstract establishes a shared wireless-suture idea. It does not support rankings of sensitivity, reading range, durability or recovery prediction across these different tasks.
