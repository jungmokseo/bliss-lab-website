Optimized Electrical Stimulation Using a Spray‐Printed Conductive Electroceutical Patch for Accelerated Wound Healing in Diabetic Mice

Jung Hyun Kim†, Soo A Kim†, Seung Hyun Lee, Young Min Jeon, Kijun Park, Minkyong Kang, Sangwon Kim, Tae Young Kim, Yeontaek Lee, Chun Gwon Park, Jungmok Seo*, Jung Seung Lee*

Advanced Healthcare Materials, 15(12), e04939 (2026)

Jungmok Seo, who now leads the BLISS Lab in the School of Electrical and Electronic Engineering at Yonsei University, is the corresponding author of this paper.

DOI: 10.1002/adhm.202504939 ↗

Why this research matters

A spray-printed poly(vinyl alcohol)–liquid-metal patch delivers electrical stimulation to wounds. The study screens stimulation conditions in macrophage experiments, then evaluates wound and inflammatory responses in mice with induced type 1 diabetes.

The problem

Wound stimulation depends on more than conductivity. Current direction, waveform, tissue contact and the timing of immune responses all matter. SCOPE connects cell-level screening of stimulation settings with a conductive patch and animal wound tests.

How the study approaches it

A sprayed PVA/liquid-metal structure deforms while maintaining electrical contact. Stimulation conditions are selected through macrophage assessments and then used with the device. The patch's mechanical compliance and the biological response to a waveform remain separate steps to validate.

What was tested

Cell experiments were followed by dorsal-wound tests in mice with STZ-induced type-1 diabetes. Waveform, exposure time and unstimulated controls are needed to distinguish patch effects from electrical stimulation. The supplementary unstimulated-patch control was mentioned in the main text but was not independently inspected in the supplementary information.

What the study found

Wound and tissue responses were accompanied by changes in immune markers. CD86 decreased significantly; the increase in CD206 was a nonsignificant trend. These marker- and time-specific results do not justify a general claim that macrophages were converted from M1 to M2 states.

What remains to be answered

The evidence applies to the tested cells and diabetic mouse wounds. Controlled, wired stimulation does not establish unrestricted wearability or benefits for people. A numerical wound-area or closure summary is not provided because the reported metric labels still need to be reconciled.

The plotted area metric is residual wound area, rather than closure percentage; lower values indicate faster repair. CD206 increases were not significant, so a demonstrated M1-to-M2 transition should not be inferred.

How it connects to other research

The related wireless closed-loop bandage combines sensing with stimulation, whereas SCOPE emphasizes selecting stimulation settings and designing the conductive interface. These approaches offer a useful system-design comparison. Different models and stimulation conditions prevent a direct ranking of healing speed.
