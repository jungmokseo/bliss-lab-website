Efficient Direct Reduction of Graphene Oxide by Silicon Substrate

Su Chan Lee, Surajit Some, Sung Wook Kim, Sun Jun Kim, Jungmok Seo, Jooho Lee, Taeyoon Lee, Jong-Hyun Ahn, Heon-Jin Choi, Seong Chan Jun*

Scientific Reports, 5, 12306 (2015)

DOI: 10.1038/srep12306 ↗

Background

Silicon is used to reduce graphene oxide and form thin films. The abstracts describe silicon nanosheets and wafers acting as reducing agents or film-forming templates, avoiding impurities from conventional chemical reductants.

What the study found

Silicon nanosheets reduce graphene oxide (GO), while silicon wafers act as templates for reduction and film formation.

The approach produces reduced graphene oxide (rGO) films while avoiding impurities associated with conventional chemical reducing agents.

Questions and limits

A question for further study is how residual oxygen, species at the silicon interface and film uniformity affect electrical performance.

How to compare the results

Compare reduction extent, film thickness, substrate treatment, and the measurement conditions for sheet resistance and optical transmission.

Related reading

Large-area ultrathin films of reduced graphene oxide as a transparent and flexible electronic material

DOI: 10.1038/nnano.2008.83 ↗

The related paper’s abstract links the thickness of solution-deposited rGO films to optoelectronic behaviour. It adds a film-formation and thickness-control perspective to this reducing-template approach.

Differences in reduction chemistry and thickness prevent attributing conductivity differences to silicon reduction alone.
