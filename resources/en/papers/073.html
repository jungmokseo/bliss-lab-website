Bioactivated lubricant-infused surfaces: A dual-action strategy for enhancing osseointegration and preventing implant-associated infections

Jae Park, Daun Jeong, Yeontaek Lee, Kijun Park, Tae Young Kim, Ji Hye Choi, Woo Young Jang*, Jungmok Seo*

Chemical Engineering Journal, 482, 149043 (2024)

DOI: 10.1016/j.cej.2024.149043 ↗

Research context and placement

The read main text combines lubricant-interface antifouling with osteoblast response to immobilized BMP-2. This supports the host–material interface category; rabbit bone endpoints and separate noninoculated pull-out conditions remain distinct.

Research background

Lubricant-infused surfaces reduce fouling, while a bone-contacting implant also needs osteoblast attachment and bone formation. This study asks whether an antifouling interface can retain those functions while selectively supporting bone-related cell responses. Fouling control and osseointegration are evaluated as distinct requirements of the same surface.

Approach and advances

BILS combines alkali-etched titanium, partial fluorination with TPFS, amine functionalization with APTES, immobilized BMP-2 and subsequent PFPE lubricant infusion. Under the tested conditions, osteoblasts were captured and spread, while slightly adherent fibroblasts remained rounded without spreading. This is neither universal cell capture nor complete elimination of fibroblast adhesion. The design seeks to combine the antifouling function of LOIS with a bone-related cellular response.

Evaluation and conditions

Cell cultures were examined for attachment and spreading, with ALP evaluated at days 7 and 14 and calcium at days 21 and 28. The bone study randomized 16 six-month-old male New Zealand white rabbits to uninfected bare negative controls, infected bare positive controls, LOIS and BILS. Implants other than negative controls were preincubated with MRSA for 12 hours, and X-ray, micro-CT and histological endpoints were assessed at day 28. Pull-out testing used three additional rabbits—one each for bare, LOIS and BILS—without bacterial preincubation. Its mechanical results must therefore remain separate from the 16-animal infection experiment.

Key findings

Osteoblast attachment, spreading and differentiation-related measurements support evaluation of the surface's cell selectivity. Bone volume, bone–implant contact (BIC) and related endpoints were used to assess integration, which the authors report as improved for BILS over LOIS. In the separate noninoculated pull-out experiment, BILS also exceeded LOIS but remained below uninfected bare titanium. That result must not be recast as direct validation of mechanical integration after infection, and complete tissue osseointegration is not established for any group.

Limits and open questions

The MRSA-prechallenged animal study in Methods §2.12 and the noninoculated pull-out experiment in §2.15 involve different conditions and animals. Although integration and mechanical results appear together in the narrative at the end of §3.6, they must not be combined into a claim that implant attachment strength was validated after infection. With one additional rabbit per pull-out group, specimen counts and animal counts must remain distinct. The 28-day rabbit model, lack of demonstrated complete tissue osseointegration, and unread SI/raw data limit interpretation and do not establish human clinical efficacy or long-term safety.

Connections to related work

The LOIS control provides an internal comparison of the design trade-off between an antifouling lubricant interface and osteoblast response. The existing related-work record on gentamicin/BMP-2-delivering titanium (DOI 10.1016/j.bone.2012.01.007) is retained only as an abstract-level precedent for evaluating antibacterial and osseointegration objectives together. Its original article and SI were not reread here. Results from different materials, infection designs and animal conditions are not directly ranked, and clinical superiority of BILS is not claimed.
