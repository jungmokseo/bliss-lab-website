Universal hydrogel adhesives with robust chain entanglement for bridging soft electronic materials

Yejin Jo†, Yurim Lee†, Jeong Hyun Heo, Yeonzu Son, Tae Young Kim, Kijun Park, Soye Kim, Seo Jung Kim, Yoonhee Jin, Seongjun Park, Jungmok Seo*

npj Flexible Electronics, 8, 39 (2024)

Jungmok Seo, who now leads the BLISS Lab in the School of Electrical and Electronic Engineering at Yonsei University, is the corresponding author of this paper.

DOI: 10.1038/s41528-024-00327-x ↗

Why this research matters

Dried poly(vinyl alcohol)–tannic-acid adhesive layers join different soft-electronic materials. Water-dependent polymer-chain mobility enables dry–dry and hydrogel–dry assembly, with tests of how the bonded interfaces behave during deformation.

Bonding dissimilar materials in soft electronics

Soft electronics bring together hydrogels, elastomers and electrodes, but the device can fail if those materials separate. dHAPT addresses the joining layer between components. It offers an assembly approach without requiring each component material to be redesigned.

How the study approaches it

The approach uses dried PVA–tannic-acid layers whose chains become mobile in the presence of water. Joining two dry materials and joining wet to dry materials begin from different states. Dry–dry assembly includes a heating step to remove water, so the two methods do not use identical, instantaneous bonding conditions.

What was tested

Lap-shear and T-peel tests load a bond in different ways. Comparisons need the material pair, thickness, hydration and assembly conditions. Biocompatibility and device demonstrations add evidence about use, but are not substitutes for bond-strength measurements.

What the study found

In the wet–dry test, a hydrogel on dHAPT-treated Ecoflex stretched with the substrate and stayed bonded even when the specimen broke. On untreated Ecoflex, the gel detached instead. This shows how water-mediated joining can transfer deformation between components, while leaving the lifetime of other material pairs open.

What remains to be answered

Repeated deformation and water exchange need further study. Acute animal demonstrations do not establish chronic implantation safety, and differing pretreatments and adhesive thicknesses complicate strength comparisons. Supplementary information and raw data were not comprehensively reviewed.

How it connects to other research

Wirthl and colleagues use cyanoacrylate dispersions that polymerize at wet interfaces, a different route from rehydrating dHAPT layers. Their inspected text includes a primer for some silicone substrates. Comparing bond strength therefore requires matched pretreatment, gel composition and test geometry.
