A Highly Sensitive Hydrogen Sensor with Gas Selectivity Using a PMMA Membrane-Coated Pd Nanoparticle/Single-Layer Graphene Hybrid

Juree Hong, Sanggeun Lee, Jungmok Seo, Soonjae Pyo, Jong-back Kim, Taeyoon Lee*

ACS Applied Materials & Interfaces, 7 (6), 3554-3561 (2015)

Jungmok Seo, who now leads the BLISS Lab in the School of Electrical and Electronic Engineering at Yonsei University, is a co-author of this paper.

DOI: 10.1021/am5073645 ↗

Background

A polymer membrane controls gas access to palladium nanoparticles on graphene for selective hydrogen sensing. The abstract reports suppressed responses to the tested methane, carbon monoxide and nitrogen dioxide.

What the study found

Galvanic deposition forms palladium nanoparticles on graphene. A PMMA polymer overlayer then selectively regulates hydrogen access.

The abstract reports hydrogen sensing with suppressed responses to the methane, carbon monoxide and nitrogen dioxide tested.

Questions and limits

An open question is whether selectivity and recovery persist under humidity, mixed gases and aging of the PMMA layer.

How to compare the results

Compare gas concentration, humidity, temperature, membrane thickness, baseline resistance, and response and recovery definitions.
