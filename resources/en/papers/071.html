Functionalized EGaIn Electrodes with Tunable Reduced‐Graphene‐Oxide Assembled EGaIn Core–Shell Particles for Soft and Deformable Electrochemical Biosensors

Dongwook Lee, Sunghyun Park, Jungmok Seo, Won-Yong Lee, Min-gu Kim*, Jayoung Kim*

Advanced Functional Materials, 34(31), 2311696 (2024)

Jungmok Seo, who now leads the BLISS Lab in the School of Electrical and Electronic Engineering at Yonsei University, is a co-author of this paper.

DOI: 10.1002/adfm.202311696 ↗

Why this research matters

Liquid-metal particles functionalized with reduced graphene oxide, platinum and gold form a deformable electrochemical electrode. The study evaluates analyte detection in buffer and electrode responses after repeated stretching, rather than establishing in-vivo sensing performance.

What the study did

The study attaches EGaIn liquid-metal particles functionalized with reduced graphene oxide (rGO), platinum and gold to a liquid-metal collector. It tests analyte sensing in buffers and electrochemical responses after repeated stretching.

The functional surface improves charge transfer that is otherwise limited by the oxide layer. The experiments also use cyclic-voltammetry (CV) preconditioning to stabilize the electrode.

What remains to be answered

Buffer-based tests leave questions about interference from real biofluids, reproducible preconditioning and long-term performance in the body.

What to compare

Compare preconditioning, analyte, electrolyte, reference electrode, active area, metal/enzyme functionalization and deformation history.

Related research

Fully solution processed liquid metal features as highly conductive and ultrastretchable conductors

DOI: 10.1038/s41528-021-00123-x ↗

The external publisher abstract describes solution-patterned liquid-metal interconnects and an LED demonstration. The present study focuses on an electrochemically active electrode surface.

Conductivity and stretchability differ from analytical selectivity. An interconnect demonstration cannot establish biosensor performance.
