Intelligent In situ Printing of Multimaterial Bioinks for First‐Aid Wound Care Guided by Eye‐In‐Hand Robot Technology

Seol-Ha Jeong, Jihyun Kim, Brendan Craig Thibault, Javier Alejandro Lozano Soto, Fatima Tourk, Joshua Steakelum, Diego Azuela, Violeta Carvalho, Guillermo Quiroga-Ocaña, Weida Zhuang, Mei Li L. Cham-Pérez, Lucia L. Huang, Zhuqing Li, Eleftheria‐Angeliki Valsami, Enya Wang, Nelson Rodrigues, Senhorinha F.C.F. Teixeira, Yuhan Lee, Jungmok Seo, Aristidis Veves, Shabir Hassan, Georgios Theocharidis*, Lance Fiondella*, Su Ryon Shin*

Advanced Materials Technologies, 9(11), 2400060 (2024)

Jungmok Seo, who now leads the BLISS Lab in the School of Electrical and Electronic Engineering at Yonsei University, is a co-author of this paper.

DOI: 10.1002/admt.202400060 ↗

Why this research matters

A robot-mounted depth camera guides direct printing of multiple bioinks onto wounds at different locations and angles. The abstract describes extrusion and spray printing alongside porous microgels designed to support cell infiltration.

What the study did

INSIGHT uses a depth camera on a robot arm to recognize wounds at different positions and angles, then print several bioinks directly at the wound.

The approach combines extrusion and spraying to address printing time over large wounds, and uses porous microgels to support cell infiltration.

What remains to be answered

The abstract mentions in-vivo evaluation, but does not establish use in emergency patients, infection management or safety under arbitrary movement.

What to compare

Comparisons need matched wound area, depth and movement, ink viscosity and cell loading. Targeting accuracy, treatment time and tissue repair should be assessed separately.

Related research

In Situ Bioprinting of Autologous Skin Cells Accelerates Wound Healing of Extensive Excisional Full-Thickness Wounds

DOI: 10.1038/s41598-018-38366-w ↗

Albanna and colleagues' abstract describes image-guided delivery of layers of autologous fibroblasts and keratinocytes. INSIGHT emphasizes robotic viewpoints and the use of different inks and printing modes.

Delivering cells for skin reconstruction and supporting infiltration through microgels are different treatment approaches, so healing effects cannot be directly ranked.
