Textile-Based Electronic Components for Energy Applications: Principles, Problems, and Perspective

Vishakha Kaushik, Jaehong Lee, Juree Hong, Seulah Lee, Sanggeun Lee, Jungmok Seo, Chandreswar Mahata, Taeyoon Lee*

Nanomaterials, 5(3), 1493-1531 (2015)

Jungmok Seo, who now leads the BLISS Lab in the School of Electrical and Electronic Engineering at Yonsei University, is a co-author of this paper.

DOI: 10.3390/nano5031493 ↗

Background

This review surveys conductive textiles for energy harvesting and storage, covering component fabrication, operating principles and limitations. It connects textile flexibility and conductivity with energy-device design.

What the review covers

This review covers conductive textiles and electronic-component fabrication, alongside the principles and limitations of textile-based energy harvesting and storage.

It connects textile flexibility and conductivity with the design of energy devices.

Questions and limits

A question for further study is whether wearability and usable power delivery can be maintained when harvesting and storage components are included.

How to compare the results

Compare area- or mass-normalization, input stimulus, storage conditions, and textile deformation.

Related reading

Highly stretchable electric circuits from a composite material of silver nanoparticles and elastomeric fibres

DOI: 10.1038/nnano.2012.206 ↗

The related paper’s abstract describes conductive paths during stretching and circuit fabrication in silver-particle/SBS fibre mats. It provides a concrete example of the review’s conductive-textile topic.

The mat’s conductivity does not establish the performance of a complete harvesting or storage system.
