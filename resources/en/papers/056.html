PUBLICATION 056 / 2020Summary based on the main text

Lubricant-infused directly engraved nano-microstructures for mechanically durable endoscope lens with anti-biofouling and anti-fogging properties

Yeontaek Lee, Yong Woo Chung, Jaeho Park, Kijun Park, Youngmin Seo, Seung-No Hong, Seung Hoon Lee, Hojeong Jeon*, Jungmok Seo*

Scientific Reports, 10, 17454 (2020)

Jungmok Seo, who now leads the BLISS Lab in the School of Electrical and Electronic Engineering at Yonsei University, is the corresponding author of this paper.

DOI: 10.1038/s41598-020-74517-8 ↗

The question behind this study

A lubricant-filled micro/nanotexture engraved directly into an endoscope lens is designed to reduce blood fouling and fogging. Tests examine optical performance, wear and sterilization durability, with lubricant replenishment included in repeated sterilization.

Keeping endoscope lenses clear and durable

An antifouling endoscope lens must also transmit light and withstand repeated contact and sterilization. This study engraves the lens itself to address particle-coating detachment, evaluating optical performance alongside surface behavior and durability.

An engraved lens and a lubricating layer

Laser-engraved structures receive a fluorinated layer and lubricant. Comparing lubricant transmission led to a liquid with a refractive index close to the lens. The engraved lens provides the support, the fluorinated surface helps retain lubricant, and the liquid contributes to both repellency and optical performance.

Separate tests for visibility, wear and sterilization

Tests examine optical performance, blood contamination, condensation, tape peeling, abrasion and sterilization. Visibility tests and damage tests measure different outcomes. Sterilization includes temperature, pressure and lubricant replenishment, so its cycles cannot be combined with wear cycles into a single lifetime estimate.

Performance after damage and sterilization

In tape-peeling tests, LIDENS retained its structure and wetting properties better than the particle-based coating control. Separate before-and-after linear-abrasion tests found retained LIDENS structure and small changes in contact angle and hysteresis. Relubrication restored performance after sterilization, showing the distinct roles of the durable scaffold and replenishable liquid layer.

Use conditions include lubricant replenishment

Every sterilization cycle includes relubrication. Comparisons with use in surgery therefore need matched structure, optical conditions, loading, sterilization and lubricant-replenishment timing. These results do not establish maintenance-free operation or a complete repeated-surgery service life.

What endoscopy adds to surface repellency

SLIPS shares the principle of reducing liquid attachment through lubrication. Endoscopy adds requirements for light transmission and visibility, so the related abstract’s repellency results do not validate lens optics or recovery after sterilization.
