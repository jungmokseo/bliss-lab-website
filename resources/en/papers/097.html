Play‐Doh Inspired a Transformative, Shape‐Adaptive iHEÄLS Enabling Post‐Crosslinking Mechanical‐Biological Modulation and Its Effectiveness in Musculoskeletal Repair

Niyou Wang†, Chao Liang†, Soo A Kim†, Jessica Heline Lopes da Fonseca, María José Veana Hernandez, Zahra Rezaei, Alvaro Dario Martinez Blanco, Nicole Joy Bassous, Da Seul Kim, Yejin Jo, Tae Young Kim, Yipei Yang, Ziqi Huang, Siyuan Chen, Feiming Li, Marcos Akira dAvila, June-Seo Kim, Misun Kang, Jungmok Seo, Sang Jin Lee*, Su Ryon Shin*

Advanced Materials, e74385 (2026)

DOI: 10.1002/adma.74385 ↗

Research context and placement

Publisher-abstract evidence for defect filling, tissue adhesion and bone repair directly fits tissue–material contact and repair. Cell migration and osteogenic modulation provide a secondary host-interaction link.

Research overview and key contributions

CNC-based triple-network iHEÄLS combines defect filling, tissue adhesion and shape recovery; the abstract reports evaluation in a bone-regeneration model.

Post-crosslinking mechanical training is used to tune the network and biological responses.

Open challenges

Editorial question: How do training history and degradation affect sustained support and regeneration? Clinical efficacy was not verified.

Comparison context

Match defect geometry, tissue/hydration, training direction/cycles, autologous bone mixing and adhesion/regeneration assessment times.

Related external research

Tough adhesives for diverse wet surfaces

The PMC abstract combines an adhesive surface with a dissipative matrix, contrasting this division of interfacial bonding and bulk dissipation with #97's training and defect filling.

This compares wet-adhesion design, not bone-regeneration performance; adhesion energy and bone-formation endpoints are distinct.
