Bioinspired Geometry‐Switchable Janus Nanofibers for Eye‐Readable H2 Sensors

Heetak Han, Sangyul Baik, Borui Xu, Jungmok Seo, Sanggeun Lee, Sera Shin, Jaehong Lee, Ja Hoon Koo, Yongfeng Mei, Changhyun Pang*, Taeyoon Lee*

Advanced Functional Materials, 27 (29), 1701618 (2017)

Jungmok Seo, who now leads the BLISS Lab in the School of Electrical and Electronic Engineering at Yonsei University, is a co-author of this paper.

DOI: 10.1002/adfm.201701618 ↗

The question behind this study

Hydrogen-responsive Janus nanofibers change shape to produce a visible optical signal. The sensor uses changes in light transmission to reveal hydrogen exposure without electrical measurement equipment.

What the study shows

Hydrogen changes the shape of Janus nanofibers, producing a visible optical signal.

The readout uses a polarizer to show hydrogen exposure through changes in light transmission, without electrical measurement equipment.

Open questions

How stable is visual interpretation under humidity, mixed gases and repeated exposure?

Conditions for comparison

Comparisons need matched hydrogen concentrations, carrier gases, array geometry, illumination and definitions of response time.
