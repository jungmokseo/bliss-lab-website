Guided Transport of Water Droplets on Superhydrophobic–Hydrophilic Patterned Si Nanowires

Jungmok Seo, Soonil Lee, Jaehong Lee, Taeyoon Lee*

ACS Applied Materials & Interfaces, 3 (12), 4722-4729 (2011)

DOI: 10.1021/am2011756 ↗

Background

Ultraviolet treatment creates water-attracting tracks on a water-repellent silicon-nanowire surface, guiding droplets along chosen paths. Resetting the tracks requires recoating and another UV treatment.

What the study found

Selective ultraviolet exposure breaks down a DTS coating on silicon nanowires, leaving water-wetting tracks that guide droplets.

The route relies on a difference in wettability between the track and its surroundings. Changing the route requires recoating and another UV treatment.

Questions and limits

The study does not establish switching in both directions with UV alone or equivalent transport of biological fluids.

How to compare the results

Compare droplet volume and surface tension, surface tilt, track width, and contact-angle hysteresis.
