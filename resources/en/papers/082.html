Large Scale Ultrafast Manufacturing of Wireless Soft Bioelectronics Enabled by Autonomous Robot Arm Printing Assisted by a Computer Vision‐Enabled Guidance System for Personalized Wound Healing

Jihyun Kim†, Seol-Ha Jeong†, Brendan Craig Thibault, Javier Alejandro Lozano Soto, Hiroyuki Tetsuka, Surya Varchasvi Devaraj, Estefania Riestra de la Rosa, Yeongseok Jang, Jeong Wook Seo, Rafael Alejandro Cornejo Rodriguez, Lucia L. Huang, Yuhan Lee, Ioana Preda, Sameer Sonkusale, Lance Fiondella, Jungmok Seo*, Lorenzo Pirrami*, Su Ryon Shin*

Advanced Healthcare Materials, 14(1), 2401735 (2025)

DOI: 10.1002/adhm.202401735 ↗

Why this research matters

This workflow uses wound-shape imaging to guide sequential printing of electrodes, a receiving coil and a soft substrate with different inks. The manually assisted design process connects to wireless electrical stimulation, with biological evaluation in fibroblast and endothelial-cell cultures.

The problem

Wound shape affects where electrodes and receiving coils can fit. CARE starts its fabrication process with a measurement of that shape. To judge the value of customization, geometry recognition must be considered alongside the stimulation ultimately delivered.

How the study approaches it

Depth imaging guides electrode and coil design, followed by sequential robotic printing with different inks. Wound data are transferred manually to MATLAB during this process. The workflow links shape measurement to fabrication, but the demonstrated design process is not fully autonomous.

What was tested

Wireless coupling depends on coil geometry, distance and electrical load. Simulated tissue fields, cell proliferation, migration and vascular assays evaluate different steps. A simulated field does not measure the dose inside an animal, and cell-culture results do not establish animal wound closure.

What the study found

Stimulated fibroblasts and endothelial cells migrated more than unstimulated controls, and endothelial tube assays showed more junctions. Migration and tube assays used a 20% duty cycle with different exposure times; proliferation and other measurements used separate settings. These are cell-level results, not evidence of animal wound closure or an optimal treatment for each patient.

What remains to be answered

Manual data transfer and the external excitation hardware remain practical constraints. Movement-dependent coupling and reproducible stimulation dose also require validation. Biological conclusions remain limited to the inspected fibroblast and endothelial-cell tests, without established clinical treatment efficacy.

How it connects to other research

Jiang and colleagues' closed-loop bandage combines sensing and stimulation with animal wound evaluation. CARE focuses on adapting fabrication to wound geometry. Animal healing outcomes and cell-migration assays answer different questions and cannot support a direct healing-rate ranking.
