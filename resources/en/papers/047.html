Full length histone H3 conjugated electrochemical biosensor for extracellular proteolytic Cathepsin L activity detection

Tae Wha Seong*, Jungmok Seo*, Kwan Hyi Lee*

Sensors & Actuators B: Chemical, 267, 237-244 (2018)

Jungmok Seo, who now leads the BLISS Lab in the School of Electrical and Electronic Engineering at Yonsei University, is the corresponding author of this paper.

DOI: 10.1016/j.snb.2018.04.040 ↗

The question behind this study

A sensor uses surface-bound histone H3 to turn cleavage by the protease cathepsin L into an electrical signal. Tests used buffer and cell-culture media; diagnostic validation in patient samples was not included.

Measuring enzyme activity

Cathepsin L (CTSL) abundance and its protein-cleaving activity are different measurement targets. This study uses cleavable full-length histone H3 to turn enzyme activity into an electrical signal. Cancer diagnosis and prognosis motivate the work, but patient-sample performance was not validated.

Reading histone cleavage electrically

Biotinylated full-length histone H3 attaches to an ITO extended gate through streptavidin–biotin binding. A field-effect transistor (FET) reads surface-charge changes associated with cleavage. Separating the sensing gate from the transistor allows measurements with multiple gates, without establishing unlimited regeneration or continual reuse of one gate.

Tests in buffer and culture medium

AFM and BCA characterize substrate attachment, distinct from cross-protease selectivity. Experiments comprise CTSL-reagent buffer reactions, calibrations in CTSL-spiked RPMI/DMEM, and LNCaP/PANC-1 culture-media measurements. Culture-media FET measurements and cell-lysate ELISA or western blots use different matrices; their absolute values cannot be treated as a matched-matrix validation.

Interpreting medium-dependent signals

The paper reports electrical responses with the histone substrate in buffer and medium-referenced, self-normalized detection in two cell-line culture media. Medium pH, ionic environment and gate-dependent baselines are central to interpretation. Its internal MR-FRFR substrate and fluorometric comparisons apply to the tested conditions and do not establish general superiority over all fluorometric assays or other sensors.

Selectivity and detection-limit questions

Supplementary information, replicate counts and the statistical basis of the detection limit are outside this account’s scope. Differences in reported fragment charges and culture-medium amount/concentration units remain unresolved. The available Methods and Results do not establish cross-protease selectivity or interference resistance in complex patient samples. Cleavage-site explanations rely on earlier work rather than new mass-spectrometric validation.

A different recognition strategy

The related abstract describes antibody-functionalized silicon-nanowire FETs for salivary IL-8 and TNF-α. It offers a comparison between antibody recognition and histone-substrate activity sensing. Different targets, samples and measured quantities prevent sensitivity or diagnostic-performance rankings.
