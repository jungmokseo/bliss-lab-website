Full length histone H3 conjugated electrochemical biosensor for extracellular proteolytic Cathepsin L activity detection

Tae Wha Seong*, Jungmok Seo*, Kwan Hyi Lee*

Sensors & Actuators B: Chemical, 267, 237-244 (2018)

DOI: 10.1016/j.snb.2018.04.040 ↗

Research context and placement

The read final published text concerns protease-activity sensing through histone-functionalized extended-gate FETs. Component foundations remains a provisional placement; interconnect–chip joints and patient diagnosis were not validated.

Boundary case: assigned to the closest interface for navigation. Read the rationale and cross-links together; the primary theme does not delimit the study. Placement of foundational work does not establish direct bioelectronic application.

Research background

CTSL abundance and proteolytic activity are different measurement targets. The study uses cleavable full-length histone H3 as an electrical-sensor substrate to link activity with a signal rather than measuring binding alone. Cancer diagnosis and prognosis motivate the work but are not validated in patient samples.

Approach and advances

Biotinylated full-length histone H3 is attached to an ITO extended gate through streptavidin–biotin binding; the FET reads proteolysis-associated surface-charge changes. Separating the replaceable sensing gate from the FET allows multiple gate measurements with one transistor. This does not establish unlimited regeneration or continual reuse of the same gate.

Evaluation and conditions

AFM and BCA characterize substrate attachment, distinct from cross-protease selectivity. Experiments comprise CTSL-reagent buffer reactions, calibrations in CTSL-spiked RPMI/DMEM, and LNCaP/PANC-1 culture-media measurements. Culture-media FET measurements and cell-lysate ELISA or western blots use different matrices; their absolute values cannot be treated as a matched-matrix validation.

Key findings

The paper reports electrical responses with the histone substrate in buffer and medium-referenced, self-normalized detection in two cell-line culture media. Medium pH, ionic environment and gate-dependent baselines are central to interpretation. Its internal MR-FRFR substrate and fluorometric comparisons apply to the tested conditions and do not establish general superiority over all fluorometric assays or other sensors.

Limits and open questions

SI, replicate counts and the statistical basis of the detection limit were not verified in this review. Internal differences in fragment-charge values and culture-media amount/concentration units are neither harmonized nor converted. Cross-protease reactivity and complex patient-matrix interference were not established in the inspected Methods/Results. Cleavage-site explanations cite prior work and are not new mass-spectrometric validation in this study.

Connections to related work

The existing abstract-only comparator uses antibody-functionalized SiNW FETs for salivary IL-8/TNF-α. It offers a recognition-strategy connection to histone-substrate activity sensing. Different targets, matrices and measured quantities prevent sensitivity or diagnostic-performance rankings; the external main text is not newly claimed read.
