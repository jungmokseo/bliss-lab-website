Reversible wettability control of silicon nanowire surfaces: From superhydrophilicity to superhydrophobicity

Jungmok Seo, Soonil Lee, Heetak Han, Youngwon Chung, Jaehong Lee, Sung-Dae Kim, Young-Woon Kim, Sangwoo Lim, Taeyoon Lee*

Thin Solid Films, 527, 179-185 (2013)

Jungmok Seo, who now leads the BLISS Lab in the School of Electrical and Electronic Engineering at Yonsei University, is a co-author of this paper.

DOI: 10.1016/j.tsf.2012.12.010 ↗

Background

Heat treatment and changes in surface chemistry control whether silicon nanowires attract or repel water. The study also examines how the surface becomes wettable again in air.

What the study found

Repeated annealing in oxygen produces superhydrophobic silicon nanowires that can hold onto water droplets.

The study explains resetting of wettability through changes in silanol and siloxane groups and a return toward hydrophilicity during exposure to air.

Questions and limits

High processing temperatures and changes during air exposure constrain possible uses. Contact angles alone do not establish antifouling performance or biocompatibility.

How to compare the results

Compare the time after treatment, surrounding atmosphere, droplet-contact conditions, and contact-angle hysteresis.
