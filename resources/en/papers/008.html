Direct Gravure Printing of Silicon Nanowires Using Entropic Attraction Forces

Jungmok Seo, Hyonik Lee, Seulah Lee, Tae Il Lee, Jae-Min Myoung, Taeyoon Lee*

Small, 8 (10), 1614-1621 (2012)

Jungmok Seo, who now leads the BLISS Lab in the School of Electrical and Electronic Engineering at Yonsei University, is a co-author of this paper.

DOI: 10.1002/smll.201102367 ↗

Background

Silicon nanowires align at engraved corners and are transferred onto flexible substrates by gravure printing. The process is demonstrated in flexible field-effect transistors.

What the study found

Evaporation-driven flow and entropic attraction align silicon nanowires at the corners of engraved grooves before printing them onto flexible substrates.

The study combines alignment with gravure transfer to demonstrate a flexible field-effect transistor (FET).

Figure 1

The figure shows nanowires aligning in microgrooves, followed by PDMS-assisted removal of unwanted wires, pickup and transfer to a flexible substrate. View this figure in the original paper. It is not reproduced here because permission to reuse it has not been confirmed.

Questions and limits

Selective transfer does not establish the device’s lifetime under repeated bending or the manufacturing yield of a complete circuit.

How to compare the results

Compare solvent polarity, nanowire size and concentration, groove geometry, and the method used to extract FET characteristics.

Related reading
