PUBLICATION 076 / 2024Summary based on the main text

Autonomously Self‐healing, Adhesive, and Stretchable Triboelectric Nanogenerator Using Multifunctional Hydrogel‐Elastomer Double Layer with a Power Management Circuit

Jinseok Oh, Minkyong Kang, Jae Park, Tae Young Kim, Kijun Park*, Jungmok Seo*

Advanced Electronic Materials, 10(8), 2400013 (2024)

Jungmok Seo, who now leads the BLISS Lab in the School of Electrical and Electronic Engineering at Yonsei University, is the corresponding author of this paper.

DOI: 10.1002/aelm.202400013 ↗

Why this research matters

A hydrogel electrode and elastomer triboelectric layer convert movement into electrical output, which a power-management circuit collects for storage. With the same capacitor and charging interval, the managed circuit stored more energy than direct charging.

The problem

A triboelectric generator turns movement into electricity, but a high open-circuit voltage does not tell us how much usable energy reaches a device. Low-frequency motion and high impedance make storage and delivery important. This study pairs a compliant generator with a power-management circuit.

How the study approaches it

The hydrogel electrode and Ecoflex layer form the flexible generator. A small temporary capacitor collects energy; when a threshold is reached, switching transfers it through the conversion circuit to output storage. Hydrogel recovery and controlled energy transfer address different parts of the system.

What was tested

Generator output must be compared under matched motion and load, and stored energy under matched capacitance and charging time. The paper compares direct charging with managed charging. LED and touch/Morse-code demonstrations show possible uses, without establishing the energy budget of every continuously operating device. Adhesion and interface tests included porcine skin, while the wearable demonstrations used VHB film in direct contact with the skin.

What the study found

Over one minute, the same 100 µF capacitor gained 1.06 µJ with direct charging and 2.93 µJ with power management. The circuit improved energy collection for that capacitor and interval. These values do not describe an equivalent increase in maximum open-circuit voltage or overall system efficiency.

What remains to be answered

Recovery of the hydrogel does not establish repair of the Ecoflex, wiring and circuit. Humidity and cycling results apply to the tested conditions. Usable energy for specific loads and performance after whole-system damage still need comparison; replacement of batteries in general is not established.

How it connects to other research

Pu and colleagues' ionic-hydrogel generator also combines stretchable energy harvesting with touch sensing. The present study brings recovery, adhesion and power management into that design problem. The external abstract cannot support peak-output rankings across different frequencies, device areas and loads.
