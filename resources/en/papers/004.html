Growth, modulation and photoresponse characteristics of vertically aligned ZnO nanowires

J.P. Kar, S.N. Das, J.H. Choi, T.I. Lee, J. Seo, T. Lee, J.M. Myoung*

Applied Surface Science, 257 (11), 4973-4977 (2011)

Jungmok Seo, who now leads the BLISS Lab in the School of Electrical and Electronic Engineering at Yonsei University, is a co-author of this paper.

DOI: 10.1016/j.apsusc.2010.12.158 ↗

Background

This study uses substrate surface layers and microscopic cavities to control the shape and density of zinc oxide nanowires for ultraviolet detection, examining how growth conditions affect their response to light.

What the study found

The study uses aluminium nitride and barium fluoride layers, along with silicon microcavities, to control zinc oxide nanowire shape and evaluate ultraviolet (UV) response.

The microcavities offer a way to change nanowire aspect ratio and density without adding an interfacial layer.

Questions and limits

The evidence concerns UV-detection devices. Selectivity in biological analysis and integration into soft devices require separate evidence.

How to compare the results

Compare wavelength, light intensity, bias voltage, device area, oxygen environment, and the definition of response time.
