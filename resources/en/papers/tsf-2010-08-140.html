Surface deformation of amorphous silicon thin film on elastomeric substrate

Sangwook Lee, Jungmok Seo, Ja Hoon Koo, Kyeong-Ju Moon, Jae-Min Myoung, Taeyoon Lee*

Thin Solid Films, 519 (2), 823-828 (2010)

Jungmok Seo, who now leads the BLISS Lab in the School of Electrical and Electronic Engineering at Yonsei University, is a co-author of this paper.

DOI: 10.1016/j.tsf.2010.08.140 ↗

Background

Thermal compression creates wavy and herringbone wrinkles in a thin amorphous-silicon film on soft silicone rubber. The study examines the height and spacing of these wrinkles to understand how the film deforms.

What the study found

Thermal compression creates wavy and herringbone wrinkles in amorphous silicon films on PDMS, an elastic polymer.

The study compares wrinkle amplitude and wavelength with a deformation model developed for crystalline silicon.

Questions and limits

Control over wrinkle shape does not establish that an electrical connection survives repeated deformation.

How to compare the results

Compare film thickness and modulus, prestrain, measurement location, and bonding to the substrate.
