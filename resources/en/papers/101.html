PUBLICATION 101 / 2026Summary based on selected sections

Bridging IC chips and deformable circuits: A packaging framework for high-density soft electronics

Yurim Lee, Yeontaek Lee, Siyeon Park, Hyein Ham, Hanbit Jang, Yunmin Jung, Sooin Lee, Jungmok Seo*

Materials Science and Engineering R: Reports, 171, 101285 (2026)

Jungmok Seo, who now leads the BLISS Lab in the School of Electrical and Electronic Engineering at Yonsei University, is the corresponding author of this paper.

DOI: 10.1016/j.mser.2026.101285 ↗

Why this research matters

This review examines architectures and packaging methods for connecting rigid integrated-circuit chips to deformable circuits. It brings fine-pitch attachment, electrical isolation, adhesion and deformation tolerance into a framework for selecting assembly methods around tissue mechanics and device functions.

The problem

Soft electronics need to follow tissue movement and curvature, while many functions still rely on rigid integrated-circuit (IC) chips. Connecting those chips to deformable circuits requires both stable electrical contacts and a structure that accommodates movement. The review brings these system and packaging needs together.

How the study approaches it

The review distinguishes rigid circuit boards enclosed in soft materials, soft electrode front ends connected to rigid back ends, and stretchable circuits containing rigid IC islands. Each accommodates movement in a different place. As integration becomes denser, interconnects and fine-pitch chip attachment can become limiting factors, requiring alignment, electrical contact and isolation to be designed together.

What was tested

Packaging needs tests of adhesion, electrical-junction stability and deformation tolerance. The strain values in Tables 2–4 refer to different component levels and to either monotonic or cyclic loading. Resistance changes, shorts and delamination must therefore be read with their test conditions; maximum strain alone cannot rank joining methods.

What the study found

The framework in §4.4 begins with the target tissue's compliance and the device's functional complexity. Chip pitch, pad count, isolation and material compatibility then guide choices such as interposers or anisotropic bonding. The contribution is a way to connect device requirements with assembly constraints, rather than a universally superior joining method.

What remains to be answered

Moisture, sweat and repeated movement can act together in long-term use. Rigid chip edges and feedthroughs also challenge protective packaging. Barrier performance must be balanced with compliance, assembly and sterilization requirements; the review does not establish clinical safety or long-term durability across all devices and tissues.

How it connects to other research

The cited studies show which requirements different circuit architectures and chip-joining methods address. A stretchable interconnect alone does not establish high-density IC joint yield, isolation or durability. This summary explains the review's framework while keeping component and system evidence distinct; the cited original experiments were not independently reviewed.
