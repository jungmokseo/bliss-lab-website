Enhancement of X-ray detection by single-walled carbon nanotube enriched flexible polymer composite

Heetak Han, Sanggeun Lee, Jungmok Seo, Chandreswar Mahata, Sung Hwan Cho, A-Reum Han, Keun-Sung Hong, Joon-Ho Park, Myung-Jin Soh, Cheolmin Park, Taeyoon Lee*

Nanoscale Research Letters, 9, 610 (2014)

DOI: 10.1186/1556-276x-9-610 ↗

Background

Adding single-walled carbon nanotubes changes X-ray sensing in a flexible polymer. The abstract reports a trade-off: higher nanotube loading aids charge separation but also increases dark current and slows the response.

What the study found

Interfaces between single-walled carbon nanotubes (SWNTs) and a polymer aid charge separation under X-rays. Increasing nanotube loading also raises dark current and slows the response.

The material design therefore links gains in sensitivity to trade-offs in signal-to-noise ratio and temporal stability.

Questions and limits

An open question for further comparison is whether irradiation history, dose rate and bending change the most suitable nanotube loading.

How to compare the results

Compare X-ray spectrum and dose rate, bias voltage, detector thickness and area, and the dark-current baseline.

Related reading

Organic Semiconducting Single Crystals as Next Generation of Low‐Cost, Room‐Temperature Electrical X‐ray Detectors

DOI: 10.1002/adma.201200283 ↗

The related paper’s abstract reports direct X-ray detection at room temperature and stable dose-rate response in organic single crystals. It offers a different charge-transport material strategy from the filled polymer used here.

Crystal and composite thickness, bias and dose rate differ, so sensitivity alone cannot establish a ranking.
