Disposable Endoscope Cap Maintaining Clear Surgical Visualization under Fogging and Fouling

Kayoung Son†, Yeontaek Lee†, Kyungwon Lee, Yejin Jo, Yurim Lee, Seung Hyun Lee, Min Young Seo, Jung Seung Lee, Seung Ah Lee, Seung-No Hong, Seung Hoon Lee*, Jungmok Seo*

ACS Applied Materials & Interfaces, 18(25), 34821-34834 (2026)

DOI: 10.1021/acsami.6c04312 ↗

Why this research matters

A disposable endoscope cap combines polymer brushes with a silicone-oil interface to address fogging and blood or mucus fouling. This introduction draws on repeated-fouling and lubricant-replenishment results in the abstract and supplementary material. These sources do not establish effectiveness in patients.

The problem

Fogging, blood and mucus can obstruct the view through an endoscope. This study describes a disposable cap designed to resist those forms of fouling.

How the study approaches it

PDMS brushes and silicone oil form a lubricated interface that can be replenished. The available abstract describes this surface design; the illustration is conceptual rather than a reconstruction of the device.

What was tested

The public supplementary information includes wetting, repeated-dipping, cell and hemolysis tests. Detailed main-text methods for the ex-vivo experiments were not read, which limits interpretation of those tests.

What the study found

Representative images show retained visibility after 100 dips in blood. Conflicting directions in the axis and legend of Figure S14C prevent a reliable quantitative reading of that panel.

What remains to be answered

Other supplementary pH, contact-angle/sliding-angle and fluid labels also conflict. Replenishment, sterilization and aging results in the supplement do not establish long-term durability or safety for patients.

How it connects to other research

The external abstract for DOI 10.1038/nbt.3020 offers a related retained-liquid interface for resisting fouling. Its vascular-thrombosis outcomes do not establish an endoscope cap's resistance to fogging, blood or mucus.
