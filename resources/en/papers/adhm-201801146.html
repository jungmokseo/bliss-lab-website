Cardiac Fibrotic Remodeling on a Chip with Dynamic Mechanical Stimulation

Ming Kong, Junmin Lee, Iman K. Yazdi, Amir K. Miri, Yi-Dong Lin, Jungmok Seo, Yu Shrike Zhang, Ali Khademhosseini*, and Su Ryon Shin*

Advanced Healthcare Materials, 8 (3), 1801146 (2019)

Jungmok Seo, who now leads the BLISS Lab in the School of Electrical and Electronic Engineering at Yonsei University, is a co-author of this paper.

DOI: 10.1002/adhm.201801146 ↗

The question behind this study

An in vitro chip model applies cyclic compression and cytokines to cardiac fibroblasts embedded in a hydrogel. It examines how mechanical deformation and cell maturity relate to fibrotic behavior.

What the study shows

The chip applies cyclic compression and cytokines to cardiac fibroblasts embedded in GelMA.

It relates deformation to fibrotic phenotype while considering myofibroblast maturity.

Open questions

Prediction of human myocardial behavior and applicability to other loading modes remain unverified.

Conditions for comparison

Comparisons need matched strain direction, frequency, gel stiffness, cell maturity and cytokine conditions.

Related studies

Human cardiac fibroblasts adaptive responses to controlled combined mechanical strain and oxygen changes in vitro

DOI: 10.7554/elife.22847 ↗

The related abstract describes combined control of strain and oxygen in human cardiac fibroblasts, adding a different set of conditions from GelMA compression and cytokines.

Oxygen levels, cell origins and mechanical loading must be matched before fibrosis effects can be ranked.
