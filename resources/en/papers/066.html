Intrinsically Nonswellable Multifunctional Hydrogel with Dynamic Nanoconfinement Networks for Robust Tissue‐Adaptable Bioelectronics

Jae Park, Ju Yeon Kim, Jeong Hyun Heo, Yeonju Kim, Soo A Kim, Kijun Park, Yeontaek Lee, Yoonhee Jin, Su Ryon Shin*, Dae Woo Kim*, Jungmok Seo*

Advanced Science, 10(12), 2207237 (2023)

DOI: 10.1002/advs.202207237 ↗

Research context and placement

Nanoconfinement couples swelling control, mechanics and conductivity in a tissue-contacting hydrogel. Tissue-adaptive mechanics is the primary theme; electrical transport and adhesion are cross-cutting aspects.

Nonswelling conductive hydrogels

Functionalized carbon nanotubes and tannic-acid nanoconfinement combine swelling resistance, conductivity and recovery in a hydrogel, demonstrated in underwater EMG and ex vivo bladder-deformation measurements.

Localized energy dissipation and reversible bonds address trade-offs among softness, toughness and recovery.

Open challenges

Air drying and excess interfacial water remain limitations. Tissue-adhesion experiments included additional chemistry and removal of excess surface water.

Comparison context

Conductivity and adhesion comparisons require matched immersion medium/time, hydration and surface treatment, as well as deformation/recovery history and electrode geometry.

Related external research

Electrical bioadhesive interface for bioelectronics

DOI: 10.1038/s41563-020-00814-2 ↗

The publisher abstract describes a thin graphene composite adhesive for electrical recording and stimulation, contrasting an interface-layer strategy with #66's bulk multifunctional hydrogel.

Only the external abstract was reviewed. Thickness, electrical contacts and biological models prevent ranking by conductivity alone.
