Intrinsically Nonswellable Multifunctional Hydrogel with Dynamic Nanoconfinement Networks for Robust Tissue‐Adaptable Bioelectronics

Jae Park, Ju Yeon Kim, Jeong Hyun Heo, Yeonju Kim, Soo A Kim, Kijun Park, Yeontaek Lee, Yoonhee Jin, Su Ryon Shin*, Dae Woo Kim*, Jungmok Seo*

Advanced Science, 10(12), 2207237 (2023)

Jungmok Seo, who now leads the BLISS Lab in the School of Electrical and Electronic Engineering at Yonsei University, is the corresponding author of this paper.

DOI: 10.1002/advs.202207237 ↗

The question behind this study

A hydrogel uses a dynamic nanoconfinement network to limit swelling while retaining compliant, conductive behavior. Demonstrations include deformation sensing on an excised porcine bladder and electromyography recorded at the skin.

Softness and conduction in wet environments

Soft hydrogels can swell in water or lose conductive paths during deformation. Dimensional changes affect tissue contact and electrical connections as well as mechanics. This study addresses softness, toughness, recovery and swelling resistance together.

A reorganizable energy-dissipating network

Dynamic confinement involving functionalized carbon nanotubes (CNTs) and TA dissipates energy, while a reorganizable network connects mechanical and electrical function. Local energy dissipation aims to preserve compliance while accommodating damage. Tissue adhesion also requires separate surface treatment.

Excised-bladder and skin-EMG tests

An excised porcine bladder was artificially filled and drained to measure resistance changes with shape. Skin electromyography (EMG) tested electrical signal pickup. These demonstrations use the same material but assess different functions: deformation sensing and body-signal recording.

Deformation sensing and underwater recording

Bladder-dependent resistance changes and underwater EMG demonstrate possible uses in wet environments. They do not validate molecular detection, clinical monitoring, chronic implant tissue responses or long-term calibration stability.

Swelling, drying and adhesion conditions

Swelling resistance does not prevent drying in air. Comparisons need matched hydration, immersion, adhesion pretreatment and deformation history. The contributions of the bulk material and the surface treatment also need to be separated.

Comparison with a thin adhesive interface

Deng’s related abstract describes a thin graphene adhesive that connects recording and stimulation with wet tissue. Comparison with a bulk hydrogel needs matched thickness, contact architecture and biological models.
