A Review on Microfluidics-Based Impedance Biosensors

Yu-Shih Chen†, Chun-Hao Huang, Ping-Ching Pai, Jungmok Seo*, Kin Fong Lei*

Biosensors, 13(1), 83 (2023)

DOI: 10.3390/bios13010083 ↗

Research background

Impedance sensing depends on how samples meet electrodes. This review connects substrates with fabrication, flow and electrode geometry. The label impedance sensing includes both changes at binding interfaces and signals from cells passing through channels. Distinguishing these modes is necessary before interpreting why a study changes its substrate, electrode or flow configuration.

Approach and advances

Silicon enables micromachined electrodes but entails process requirements. Commercial PCB fabrication is mature, yet conventional linewidth/spacing constrains electrode miniaturization and sensitivity improvement. Choosing an accessible fabrication platform is not always the same as choosing the smallest electrodes. The classification exposes this manufacturing trade-off, while analyte-specific sensitivity still depends on electrode functionalization and measurement conditions.

Evaluation and conditions

Flow focusing and electrode geometry change electrical interrogation. Stationary-interface and flowing-cell measurements must be distinguished. For flowing cells, transit position relative to electrodes matters. For stationary samples, binding and interfacial measurement conditions become central; comparing their detection limits can therefore compare different targets and measurement questions.

Key findings

The review highlights paper’s low cost while retaining electrode patterning and surface binding as challenges. Substrate selection therefore connects cost with fabrication and analyte presentation. The review’s outcome is a map of linked design choices rather than a winning substrate. Even paper-based devices can target different uses through their electrodes and surface chemistry, preventing performance generalization from the substrate name alone.

Limits and open questions

Frequency, spacing, flow and target size affect comparison. Commercial prospects require device-specific user and sample validation. Future directions in a review are not validated product performance. This account adds no numerical sensitivity or throughput ranking and identifies the primary-study conditions needed before making such comparisons.

Connections to related work

Gawad grounds the flow/electrode comparison in particle measurements. Cell-flow readouts and antigen-binding detection limits are not equivalent. Individual-particle interrogation in glass/polyimide channels illustrates how flow and electrodes jointly define a reading. Its measurement unit differs from antigen binding, so the verified abstract is used to explain architecture, not to claim superior detection.
