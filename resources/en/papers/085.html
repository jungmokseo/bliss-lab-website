Bioadaptive liquid-infused multifunctional fibers for long-term neural recording via BDNF stabilization and enhanced neural interaction

Tae Young Kim†, Yeonzu Son†, Keun-Young Yook, Dong Gyu Lee, Young Kim, Seo Jung Kim, Kijun Park, Yurim Lee, Tae Kyung Lee, Justin J. Chung, Congqi Yang, Seongjun Park*, Jungmok Seo*

Science Advances, 11(37), eadz1228 (2025)

DOI: 10.1126/sciadv.adz1228 ↗

Why this research matters

Flexible neural-recording fibers combine a lubricated interface with immobilized brain-derived neurotrophic factor (BDNF) to resist nonspecific attachment while supporting neuronal interaction. Representative single-unit recordings extend to twelve months, while comparative electrode statistics cover a separate eight-month period.

Fiber probes for neural recording and tissue interaction

A neural probe must record signals while remaining in contact with tissue over time. Reducing unwanted attachment is useful, but the surface also needs to support beneficial neuronal interactions. TAB addresses that selective-contact problem on a flexible fiber probe.

How the study approaches it

TAB combines a lubricated interface with brain-derived neurotrophic factor (BDNF)-related functionality. The aim is to discourage nonspecific adhesion while supporting targeted neuronal interaction. The probe's electrical functions and its biological surface functions need separate evaluation.

What was tested

Recording duration must be interpreted with electrode counts and analysis windows. Tissue-response measurements and electrical recordings address different outcomes. The inspected Figure 6 analysis does not establish that every electrode survived or supply animal counts that were not reported.

What the study found

TAB-coated fibers yielded representative single-unit recordings up to twelve months. Comparative statistics covered a separate eight-month period: detected units per electrode were maintained for coated fibers and declined for bare fibers. These two time windows do not establish identical twelve-month performance for every electrode or a device-lifetime multiplier.

What remains to be answered

Further comparison needs electrode attrition and matched criteria for recording and identifying units. A long observation period cannot fill gaps in supplementary conditions. This summary describes the inspected results; it is not a complete reanalysis of raw recordings.

How it connects to other research

Canales and colleagues' thermally drawn fibers combine recording, optics and fluid delivery. TAB adds a focus on surface biochemistry and the surrounding tissue response. The external abstract supports this difference in design emphasis, without ranking chronic recording performance.
