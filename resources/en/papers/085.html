Bioadaptive liquid-infused multifunctional fibers for long-term neural recording via BDNF stabilization and enhanced neural interaction

Tae Young Kim†, Yeonzu Son†, Keun-Young Yook, Dong Gyu Lee, Young Kim, Seo Jung Kim, Kijun Park, Yurim Lee, Tae Kyung Lee, Justin J. Chung, Congqi Yang, Seongjun Park*, Jungmok Seo*

Science Advances, 11(37), eadz1228 (2025)

DOI: 10.1126/sciadv.adz1228 ↗

Research context and placement

Lubrication and immobilized BDNF combine resistance to nonspecific attachment with neural interaction. The focus on surface–tissue responses during chronic recording supports a host-interaction assignment.

Research overview and key contributions

TAB combines immobilized BDNF and lubrication to discourage nonspecific attachment while supporting selective neural interaction on flexible multifunctional fibers.

Biochemical interaction complements broad antifouling, with mouse recordings compared against uncoated fibers.

Open challenges

Twelve-month examples differ from the eight-month statistical comparison. Fig. 6G counts electrodes, not independent animals. Human long-term performance remains untested.

Comparison context

Editorial proposal: match brain region, insertion, geometry and spike sorting; separate TAB effects from lubrication-only and BDNF-only conditions.

Related external research

Publisher abstract and metadata were read. Canales et al. integrate recording, optics and fluid delivery in drawn fibers; TAB adds a focus on surface biochemistry and host response.
