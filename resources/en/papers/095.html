Lubricious anti-adhesive interface prevents friction, biofilm, and encrustation in long-term indwelling ureteral stents

Yejin Jo†, Yeontaek Lee†, Sungun Bang, Kayoung Son, Kijun Park, Dokyun Kim, Soo A Kim, Seonghyeon Eom, Inhee Choi, Su-Jin Shin, Kyo Chul Koo*, Jungmok Seo*

Materials Today Bio, 39, 103405 (2026)

DOI: 10.1016/j.mtbio.2026.103405 ↗

Research context and placement

A lubricated ureteral-stent interface addresses bacteria, encrustation and irritation, directly fitting device–host interaction.

Research overview and key contributions

FLUID uses the entire microporous TPU stent wall as a lubricant reservoir.

It addresses friction, early attachment and encrustation together, with a porcine ureter model.

Open challenges

The abstract reports more modest mature-biofilm effects than early-adhesion results and explicitly calls for further preclinical validation.

Comparison context

Separate early attachment from mature biofilms; align species, urine chemistry, flow, dwell time and commercial comparators.

Related external research

A bioinspired omniphobic surface coating on medical devices prevents thrombosis and biofouling

DOI: 10.1038/nbt.3020 ↗

Unlike the comparator's surface-tethered fluorinated liquid film, FLUID designs a replenishing reservoir within the wall.

Blood thrombosis and urinary mineral/biofilm endpoints differ.
