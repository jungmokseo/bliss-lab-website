PUBLICATION 060 / 2021Summary based on selected sections

A multifunctional electronic suture for continuous strain monitoring and on-demand drug release

Yeontaek Lee†, Hwajoong Kim†, Yeonju Kim, Seungbeom Noh, Beomsoo Chun, Jinho Kim, Charnmin Park, Minyoung Choi, Kijun Park, Jaehong Lee*, Jungmok Seo*

Nanoscale, 13(43), 18112-18124 (2021)

Jungmok Seo, who now leads the BLISS Lab in the School of Electrical and Electronic Engineering at Yonsei University, is the corresponding author of this paper.

DOI: 10.1039/d1nr04508c ↗

The question behind this study

An electronic suture senses strain through resistance changes and uses heat from externally supplied current to trigger drug release. Evaluation includes cell culture and excised porcine skin, without demonstrating treatment efficacy in live animals or patients.

Adding sensing and release to a suture

A suture fixes tissue, while sensing local deformation and delivering an agent require additional functions. DRESS combines strain sensing with externally powered drug release in one fiber. Integration of sensing and heating must be distinguished from clinical treatment efficacy.

A conductive core and heat-responsive shell

The structure comprises an AgNP/PU conductive core, a PDMS protective/insulating intermediate layer and a drug-loaded PVA–PNIPAm thermoresponsive outer layer. Resistance changes provide a strain readout, while externally supplied current produces Joule heating for thermal response and release. The accompanying concept omits the PDMS intermediate layer and does not reproduce the complete cross-section.

Fiber tests, cells and excised skin

Strain–resistance response, electrical heating and drug release are separate evaluation endpoints. Biological tests comprise an NIH-3T3 in vitro scratch assay and an ex vivo demonstration on excised porcine skin. Migration-related closure of a culture scratch must not be recast as wound-healing rates in living animals or patients.

Controlling heat during deformation

The main text combines strain sensing and thermally triggered release in a suture, with closed-loop voltage control compensating strain-dependent resistance and temperature changes. That control maintains heating conditions. It does not validate closed-loop therapy that diagnoses healing or drug concentration and determines a dose.

In vivo validation and use conditions

The authors identify limits in hydrophilic-drug loading and a need for in vivo validation. Heating, release, fixation, repeated deformation and long-term use need condition-matched evaluation. Supplementary information and raw data are outside this account’s scope. Externally wired demonstrations do not establish wireless or battery-free operation, and cell or excised-skin results do not establish human safety or treatment efficacy.

Comparison with a wireless suture

The related RFID capacitive-suture abstract describes deep-wound tests in living pigs. Its environment and operating method differ from DRESS’s cell and excised-skin tests. Its wireless operation and in vivo validation cannot be transferred to DRESS.

Related studies

Wirelessly operated bioelectronic sutures for the monitoring of deep surgical wounds
