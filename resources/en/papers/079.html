Hydrogel Ink for 3D Printing with High and Widely Tunable Mechanical Properties via the Salting‐Out Effect

Kun Woo Ko, Taehoon Lee, Jae-Hak Jeong, Yeontaek Lee, Ikjin Kim, Jungmok Seo, Yong-Hwa Park, Jin-Oh Kim*, Steve Park*

Advanced Materials Technologies, 9(10), 2400157 (2024)

DOI: 10.1002/admt.202400157 ↗

Research context and placement

The ink targets tissue-mimetic mechanics and vessel training models. It is provisionally foundational because these differ from direct living-tissue contact or repair experiments.

Boundary case: assigned to the closest interface for navigation. Read the rationale and cross-links together; the primary theme does not delimit the study. Placement of foundational work does not establish direct bioelectronic application.

Research overview and key contributions

Salting-out controls ink gelation to enable direct writing of hydrogels with varied stiffness.

It addresses printability–mechanics tradeoffs and demonstrates layered vessel models for suturing and electrocautery training.

Open challenges

Modulus matching does not establish vascular anisotropy, fatigue or thrombosis behavior. Training models do not establish implant performance.

Comparison context

Match salt, temperature, hydration and printing direction; distinguish tensile modulus from pressure-dependent tube deformation.
