Resealable Antithrombotic Artificial Vascular Graft Integrated with a Self-Healing Blood Flow Sensor

Kijun Park†, Soojung An†, Jihyun Kim†, Sungjun Yoon, Jihyang Song, Daekwang Jung, Jae Park, Yeontaek Lee, Donghee Son*, Jungmok Seo*

ACS Nano, 17(8), 7296–7310 (2023)

Jungmok Seo, who now leads the BLISS Lab in the School of Electrical and Electronic Engineering at Yonsei University, is the corresponding author of this paper.

DOI: 10.1021/acsnano.2c10657 ↗

The question behind this study

A self-bonding polymer joins a resealable vascular conduit with a carbon-nanotube sensor to track flow-induced pressure and deformation. Benchtop and short-term biological tests assess sealing and sensing, without establishing chronic vessel replacement or direct volumetric-flow measurement.

Combining sensing and sealing

A vascular sensor needs a reliable signal while preventing leakage at attachment points and needle punctures. This study combines sensing and sealing in one conduit, testing the functions separately.

The roles of conduit, lubricant and sensor

A healable conduit with a lubricated lumen incorporates a CNT sensor made from a related polymer. Self-bonding supports attachment and sealing, the lubricated inner surface reduces blood-facing attachment, and the CNT composite turns deformation into resistance changes.

Pressure sensing and distinct biological models

Sensor resistance tracked pressure changes produced by pump-driven flow, with leakage and signal tests before and after puncture. Ex vivo porcine-aorta connections, one-week subcutaneous implantation and three-day injured-vein wrapping examine different endpoints. The sensor does not directly measure volumetric throughput, and wrapping an injured vein differs from replacing a resected vessel segment.

Function retained after puncture

Leakage suppression and pressure sensing persisted after puncture, and no leakage was reported at ex vivo vessel connections. These findings support retention of the tested sensing and sealing functions after local damage. They do not establish prolonged, thrombus-free patency of a replacement vessel in vivo.

Chronic patency and flow calibration

Brief biological observations cannot establish chronic implant success. Comparisons need matched diameter, pressure, flow waveform and anticoagulation, with leakage, calibration and thrombosis assessed separately. Geometry and stiffness can change the pressure–resistance relationship even at the same pump setting. Clinical flow accuracy and chronic patency remain open questions.

The vascular animal experiment wraps an injured rodent vena cava and observes it for three days. It is distinct from a long-term graft-patency study or clinical safety trial.

Comparison with a wireless pulse sensor

Boutry’s related abstract describes a biodegradable wireless arterial-pulse sensor. It shares the goal of vascular monitoring but is neither a replacement vessel nor equivalent volumetric-flow calibration. This study also addresses conduit sealing, blood-facing attachment and sensor joining, so wireless operation or signal magnitude alone cannot rank the complete systems.
