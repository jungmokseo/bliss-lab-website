Ag Nanowire Reinforced Highly Stretchable Conductive Fibers for Wearable Electronics

Seulah Lee, Sera Shin, Sanggeun Lee, Jungmok Seo, Jaehong Lee, Seung-Bae Son, Hyeon Jin Cho, Hassan Algadi, Saleh Al-Sayari, Dae-Eun Kim, Taeyoon Lee*

Advanced Functional Materials, 25 (21), 3114-3121 (2015)

Jungmok Seo, who now leads the BLISS Lab in the School of Electrical and Electronic Engineering at Yonsei University, is a co-author of this paper.

DOI: 10.1002/adfm.201500628 ↗

Background

Silver nanowires bridge silver nanoparticles inside stretchable polymer fibres. The abstract reports conduction during stretching and glove-based motion sensing, with resistance changing as the fibre deforms.

What the study found

Silver nanowires bridge silver nanoparticles inside SBS polymer fibres. The abstract reports conduction during stretching and motion sensing with a glove.

The nanowire bridges help maintain connections between particles as the fibre extends. Because its resistance responds to strain, the fibre should not be described as a strain-invariant interconnect.

Questions and limits

The abstract does not establish wash durability, fatigue lifetime, contact resistance, silver release, or suitability for prolonged skin wear.

How to compare the results

Compare filler loading, fibre diameter, strain, strain rate, cycle count and initial conductivity. Deformed cross-section and resistance normalization also matter.

Related reading

Highly stretchable electric circuits from a composite material of silver nanoparticles and elastomeric fibres

DOI: 10.1038/nnano.2012.206 ↗

The related paper’s abstract describes a connected network of silver particles in an SBS fibre mat. This study adds nanowire bridges within fibres, offering a comparison of material design.

Mats and individual fibres differ in geometry and conductivity calculation, so abstract values cannot support a simple improvement ratio.
