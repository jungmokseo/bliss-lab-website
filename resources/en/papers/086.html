Encapsulation Strategies for OLEDs: From Conventional Devices to Flexible and Biointegrated Applications

Jangho Kim†, Sangwoo Park, Tae Young Kim, Yejin Jo, Kijun Park*, Jungmok Seo*

Advanced Electronic Materials, 11(19), e00475 (2025)

DOI: 10.1002/aelm.202500475 ↗

Research context and placement

OLED barrier and mechanical stability fit protective packaging. Its breadth from conventional displays to biointegration requires a qualified boundary; skin conformity is secondary.

Boundary case: assigned to the closest interface for navigation. Read the rationale and cross-links together; the primary theme does not delimit the study. Placement of foundational work does not establish direct bioelectronic application.

Research overview and key contributions

This review covers encapsulation from conventional OLEDs to flexible and biointegrated devices.

It links moisture/oxygen barriers with thermal/mechanical stability and thin-film encapsulation structures.

Open challenges

Editorial question: Does low permeation translate into device lifetime under cyclic strain and biofluid exposure?

Comparison context

Match permeation-test conditions, thickness, defects/deformation history, operating luminance and lifetime definitions.
