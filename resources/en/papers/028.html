The impact of atomic layer deposited SiO2 passivation for high-k Ta1−xZrxO on the InP substrate

Chandreswar Mahata, Il-Kwon Oh, Chang Mo Yoon, Chang Wan Lee, Jungmok Seo, Hassan Algadi, Mi-Hyang Sheen, Young-Woon Kim, Hyungjun Kim, Taeyoon Lee*

Journal of Materials Chemistry C, 3(39), 10293-10301 (2015)

Jungmok Seo, who now leads the BLISS Lab in the School of Electrical and Electronic Engineering at Yonsei University, is a co-author of this paper.

DOI: 10.1039/c5tc01890k ↗

Background

This study examined how silicon-dioxide passivation changes interfacial diffusion and electrical properties in InP MOS capacitors.

What the study found

The tested MOS capacitors used an InP substrate and a tantalum–zirconium oxide gate dielectric, with comparisons of silicon-dioxide passivation.

The local publisher article reports reduced In diffusion, capacitance frequency dispersion and leakage current after silicon-dioxide passivation. Improved interface properties are accompanied by a lower effective dielectric constant.

Questions and limits

These results are limited to the tested MOS capacitors and process and measurement conditions. The unpassivated sample cannot be reliably compared using the same conductance extraction of interface-trap density. The leakage-current figure and body use inconsistent quantity/unit labels, so the numbers are not reused as current densities. SI and the latest correction status remain unverified.

How to compare the results

A later comparison requires film thickness and composition, annealing conditions, and the frequency and bias used for capacitance–voltage measurements.
