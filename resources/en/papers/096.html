Lubricious anti-adhesive interface prevents friction, biofilm, and encrustation in long-term indwelling ureteral stents

Yejin Jo†, Yeontaek Lee†, Sungun Bang, Kayoung Son, Kijun Park, Dokyun Kim, Soo A Kim, Seonghyeon Eom, Inhee Choi, Su-Jin Shin, Kyo Chul Koo*, Jungmok Seo*

Materials Today Bio, 39, 103405 (2026)

DOI: 10.1016/j.mtbio.2026.103405 ↗

Why this research matters

A porous lubricant reservoir helps maintain a low-friction, attachment-resistant ureteral-stent surface. The study combines artificial-ureter friction tests with tissue and deposit assessments in a noninfected porcine model, which cannot by itself establish infection prevention.

Ureteral stent coatings: friction and encrustation

A ureteral stent encounters friction during insertion and removal and deposits while it remains in place. Lubrication can reduce early attachment, but the liquid may be depleted. FLUID connects the outer surface to lubricant stored within the stent wall.

How the study approaches it

A primer-stabilized silicone interface works with a porous thermoplastic-polyurethane (TPU) reservoir to replenish local lubricant loss. The principal aim is to maintain an attachment-resistant surface. Preventing early bacterial adhesion is different from killing bacteria or removing a mature biofilm.

What was tested

Pull-out tests in an artificial ureter assess friction, while porcine implantation assesses tissue responses and deposits. Tria was always on the left and FLUID on the right, with three pigs per duration. Pairing reduces between-animal variation but leaves possible laterality effects.

What the study found

Friction and early-attachment results support the lubrication approach. Calcium and magnesium differences were significant at four weeks but not at eight weeks. Neither group showed distinct biofilms in the sterile pig study, so that experiment alone cannot establish infection prevention.

What remains to be answered

FLUID and comparator stents underwent different preconditioning before in-vitro friction tests, which must accompany numerical comparisons. Lubricant distribution in the body and elimination were not quantified. Long-term infected conditions and human outcomes remain to be established.

How it connects to other research

The related omniphobic medical-device coating also retains a liquid interface, but FLUID includes a replenishing reservoir in the stent wall. Blood-contact thrombosis tests differ from urinary friction, mineral deposits and biofilms. Vascular results cannot establish safety or effectiveness in the ureter.
