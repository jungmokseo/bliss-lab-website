Immune‐Responsive Bioinks Enable Vision‐Guided in Situ Robotic Bioprinting to Accelerate Diabetic Wound Healing

Seol-Ha Jeong†, Eleftheria-Angeliki Valsami†, Ki-Tae Kim†, Kijun Park†, Jihyun Kim, Javier Alejandro Lozano Soto, Jaeseo Lee, Rafael Alejandro Cornejo Rodríguez, Danilo Martins dos Santos, Jarno Hiemstra, Mei L. L. Cham-Pérez, Montserrat Legorreta Gonzalez, Soo A Kim, Jungmok Seo, Zhuqing Li, Brendan Craig Thibault, Nebras Sobahi, Yejin Jo, Mohammad Asif Hussain, Kyungsang Kim, Lance Fiondella, Aristidis Veves, Woo-Jin Kim*, Georgios Theocharidis*, Su Ryon Shin*

Advanced Functional Materials, 36(67), e77191 (2026)

DOI: 10.1002/adfm.77191 ↗

Research context and placement

Publisher-abstract evidence addresses hypoxia, macrophage states and regenerative immune responses directly. Conformal wound printing links to tissue repair; robot use alone does not establish interconnect–chip integration.

Research overview and key contributions

A vision-guided robot deposits bioink containing oxygen-generating zein microparticles and human mesenchymal stem cells into wounds.

It combines conformal printing with hypoxia and immune modulation; the abstract's macrophage associations do not isolate causal contributions.

Open challenges

Editorial question: Which controls separate oxygen, cell and printing contributions, and establish longer-term safety?

Comparison context

Match diabetes model, wound size, oxygen release, cell dose, controls, immune-assessment timing and printing conformity.

Related external research

In Situ 3D Bioprinting Living Photosynthetic Scaffolds for Autotrophic Wound Healing

DOI: 10.34133/2022/9794745 ↗

The PubMed abstract reports wound printing with light-driven oxygen production by microalgae, offering an oxygen-source comparison with #96's particles and stem cells.

Light dependence, biological constituents and models differ; the two abstracts do not establish superior immunomodulation or wound closure.
