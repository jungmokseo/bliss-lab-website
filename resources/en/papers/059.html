A Lubricated Nonimmunogenic Neural Probe for Acute Insertion Trauma Minimization and Long‐Term Signal Recording

Yeontaek Lee†, Hyogeun Shin†, Dongwon Lee, Sungah Choi, Il-Joo Cho*, Jungmok Seo*

Advanced Science, 8(15), 2100231 (2021)

DOI: 10.1002/advs.202100231 ↗

The question behind this study

A neural probe combines a lubricated coating with electrode design to address insertion friction and electrical contact together. The study examines insertion injury and local immune responses, with repeated neural recordings in mice.

Insertion and attachment in neural recording

Neural probes can injure tissue during insertion and later accumulate proteins and cells that affect recording. LIPS uses lubrication to address friction and attachment, linking insertion tests, fouling tests and neural recordings.

Balancing lubrication and electrical contact

A lubricant layer can reduce friction while raising electrode impedance. Pt-black surface-area enhancement addresses this electrical cost. Recording performance therefore depends on electrode design as well as the coating’s biological effects.

Mouse recordings and tissue assessment

Chronic comparisons use weekly recordings from anesthetized mice and tissue immunostaining. Anesthesia reduces movement artifacts, and the Methods describe signal-sorting and stopping criteria. Sorted neural signals, animals and tissue samples are separate sampling units.

Signal retention and immune markers

Coated probes retained detectable signals longer and showed lower local immune-marker expression. Together, these observations support the surface-design interpretation. Contact impedance and analysis conditions also affect recordings, so immune responses cannot be isolated as the sole cause of signal changes.

Conditions behind recording duration

Impedance increases, selective coating and lubricant retention remain challenges. Recording durations need matched anesthesia, sorting and stopping criteria. Longer detectability does not mean every electrode continuously retained the same signal quality.

Attachment and foreign-body responses

Anderson’s review abstract explains the pathway from protein adsorption to immune responses. It supports the biological motivation for reducing attachment, while LIPS recording longevity still requires evidence from this study’s impedance, recording and anesthesia conditions.
