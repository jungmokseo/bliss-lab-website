Spatially and temporally controlled hydrogels for tissue engineering

Jeroen Leijten†, Jungmok Seo†, Kan Yue, Grissel Trujillo-de Santiago, Ali Tamayol, Guillermo U. Ruiz-Esparza, Su Ryon Shin, Roholah Sharifi, Iman Noshadi, Mario Moisés Álvarez, Yu Shrike Zhang, Ali Khademhosseini*

Materials Science and Engineering R: Reports, 119, 1-35 (2017)

Jungmok Seo, who now leads the BLISS Lab in the School of Electrical and Electronic Engineering at Yonsei University, is a co-author of this paper.

DOI: 10.1016/j.mser.2017.07.001 ↗

The question behind this study

This review examines hydrogels that organize cells in space while controlling how their biochemical environment changes over time. It connects patterning and printing with timed release and degradation for tissue engineering.

What the study shows

The review brings together spatial hydrogel patterning and time-dependent control of release and degradation for tissue engineering.

It connects surface modification, microfabrication and printing with control of changing biochemical cues.

Open questions

A key question is how to evaluate spatial and temporal control alongside preservation of cell function.

Conditions for comparison

Comparisons should distinguish gel chemistry, cell type, light exposure, release and degradation times, and the intended endpoint.
