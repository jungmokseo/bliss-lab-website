Capillary Force-Induced Glue-Free Printing of Ag Nanoparticle Arrays for Highly Sensitive SERS Substrates

Jaehong Lee, Jungmok Seo, Dayeong Kim, Sera Shin, Sanggeun Lee, Chandreswar Mahata, Hyo-Sung Lee, Byung-Wook Min, Taeyoon Lee*

ACS Applied Materials & Interfaces, 6 (12), 9053-9060 (2014)

Jungmok Seo, who now leads the BLISS Lab in the School of Electrical and Electronic Engineering at Yonsei University, is a co-author of this paper.

DOI: 10.1021/am5000382 ↗

Background

Water capillary forces transfer arrays of silver nanoparticles without added glue, creating surfaces for surface-enhanced Raman spectroscopy (SERS). Dye molecules were used to evaluate detection performance.

What the study found

Water’s capillary forces transfer lines of silver nanoparticles from a wrinkled template to a water-wetting substrate.

The method transfers the pattern without added glue, then uses probe molecules to evaluate surface-enhanced Raman spectroscopy (SERS).

Figure 1

The figure shows silver nanoparticles assembling on a buckled template and transferring to a flat substrate through water-mediated capillary forces, without glue. View this figure in the original paper. It is not reproduced here because permission to reuse it has not been confirmed.

Questions and limits

Dye detection does not establish selectivity in complex biological samples or long-term stability.

How to compare the results

Compare the laser, analyte, exposure conditions, particle spacing, substrate, and definition of the detection limit.
