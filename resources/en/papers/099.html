Bioadhesive hydrogels as immunomodulatory interfaces for chronic wound healing: from microenvironmental regulation mechanism to engineering strategies

Kayoung Son†, Soo A Kim†, Minkyong Kang, Yejin Jo, Chansoo Kim, Kijun Park, Yunmin Jung, Tae Young Kim, Jungmok Seo*

Burns & Trauma, 14, tkag055 (2026)

Jungmok Seo, who now leads the BLISS Lab in the School of Electrical and Electronic Engineering at Yonsei University, is the corresponding author of this paper.

DOI: 10.1093/burnst/tkag055 ↗

Why this research matters

This review examines how adhesive hydrogels can coordinate adhesion, deformation, transport and immune regulation at chronic wounds. It considers how inflammation, exudate and removal injury change with wound stage, shaping when and how each function is needed.

The problem

A chronic-wound dressing must do more than adhere when wet. Exudate changes the contact surface, movement loads the gel and inflammation changes over time. The review connects adhesion, mechanical integrity, transport and immune regulation at that evolving interface.

How the study approaches it

Strong attachment can make removal damaging, while excessive swelling can weaken contact. Inflammation and reactive oxygen species also play different roles at different stages. The design question is how much of each function is needed, and when.

What was tested

This is a review rather than a trial of a new device. Its framework distinguishes wound cause and stage, exudate, mechanical loading, removal injury and the timing of immune assessments. Wound closure alone cannot establish both durable contact and appropriately timed immune regulation.

What the study found

Sensors could link measurements of wound condition to adjustments in treatment. Detecting a change and responding to it safely require separate validation. The review presents a direction for adaptive care, without establishing clinical closed-loop wound treatment.

What remains to be answered

Rodent wound contraction differs from the persistent inflammation and impaired perfusion of human chronic wounds. Translation also requires reproducible manufacturing, sterilization and longer-term safety. The cited experiments and regulatory pathways were not independently examined for this summary.

How it connects to other research

The related wireless closed-loop bandage provides a specific example of sensing and stimulation. It makes the review's questions concrete: what is measured, and which treatment is controlled? Only the external abstract was checked, and one device cannot validate every hydrogel strategy in the review.
