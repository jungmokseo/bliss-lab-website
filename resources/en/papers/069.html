3D printing of mechanically tough and self-healing hydrogels with carbon nanotube fillers

Soo A Kim, Yeontaek Lee, Kijun Park, Jae Park, Soohwan An, Jinseok Oh, Minkyong Kang, Yurim Lee, Yejin Jo, Seung-Woo Cho, Jungmok Seo*

International Journal of Bioprinting, 9(5), 765 (2023)

DOI: 10.18063/ijb.765 ↗

Research context and placement

Printed stretchable conductive patterns and electrical recovery after cutting were tested. Tissue adhesion/mechanics are secondary; this subtheme includes stretchable electrodes as well as fibers.

Research overview and key contributions

CNTs are incorporated into a reversible PVA/TA/PAA network to make a printable conducting hydrogel.

The study combines printed structures with mechanical recovery and demonstrates electrical reconnection and motion sensing.

Open challenges

Demonstrations do not establish signal stability under sweat, dehydration or prolonged loading; tensile strength and fracture toughness must be distinguished.

Comparison context

Match print direction, line width, filler, hydration and damage location; assess electrical and mechanical recovery together.

Related external research

3D printing of conducting polymers

DOI: 10.1038/s41467-020-15316-7 ↗

Yuk prints PEDOT:PSS paths with insulation; the focal comparison is restoration of conductive paths after cutting a CNT composite network.

PEDOT:PSS bending and hydrogel cutting impose different damage; conductivity and healing ratios cannot be ranked directly.
