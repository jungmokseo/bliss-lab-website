Electrostatically-induced trajectory switching system on a multi-inlet-multi-outlet superhydrophobic droplet guiding track

Soonil Lee, Seulah Lee, Dayeong Kim, Jungmok Seo, Chandreswar Mahata, Hyunseok Hwang, Hassan Algadi, Saleh Al-Sayari, Youngcheol Chae*, Taeyoon Lee*

RSC Advances, 5(8), 5754-5761 (2015)

Jungmok Seo, who now leads the BLISS Lab in the School of Electrical and Electronic Engineering at Yonsei University, is a co-author of this paper.

DOI: 10.1039/c4ra13014f ↗

Background

An electrostatic system switches water-droplet paths on a water-repellent track with multiple inlets and outlets. This description is based on a one-sentence public summary, which does not establish quantitative performance.

What the study found

The available one-sentence summary describes electrostatic switching of droplet routes on a superhydrophobic track with multiple inlets and outlets.

It supports an account of the track-and-actuation concept, but does not provide enough evidence to assess quantitative performance.

Original figure

The original figure and its full caption are not sufficiently documented here, so no figure is reproduced. See the official article for the original.

Questions and limits

A question for further study is how droplet size, conductivity and electrode placement affect routing errors.

How to compare the results

A later comparison should match electric field, track geometry, liquid properties, and the definition of routing success.
