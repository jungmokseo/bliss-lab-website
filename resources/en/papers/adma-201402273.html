Switchable Water‐Adhesive, Superhydrophobic Palladium‐Layered Silicon Nanowires Potentiate the Angiogenic Efficacy of Human Stem Cell Spheroids

Jungmok Seo†, Jung Seung Lee†, Kihong Lee, Dayeong Kim, Kisuk Yang, Sera Shin, Chandreswar Mahata, Hwae bong Jung, Wooyoung Lee, Seung-Woo Cho*, Taeyoon Lee*

Advanced Materials, 26 (41), 7043-7050 (2014)

Jungmok Seo, who now leads the BLISS Lab in the School of Electrical and Electronic Engineering at Yonsei University, is a co-author of this paper.

DOI: 10.1002/adma.201402273 ↗

Background

A palladium–silicon surface with switchable droplet adhesion supports the culture of stem-cell spheroids. Their secretions and effects on vascular endothelial cells were evaluated in vitro; therapeutic effects in the body were not demonstrated.

What the study found

The study cultures stem-cell spheroids on palladium/silicon surfaces with switchable droplet adhesion. It evaluates secretion and responses in human umbilical vein endothelial cells (HUVECs).

It examines how flow within a droplet and cell aggregation relate to spheroid quality.

Figure 3

The figure shows hanging culture droplets on hydrogen-exposed palladium/silicon nanowires, with controlled spheroid size and measurements of vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) secretion. View this figure in the original paper. It is not reproduced here because permission to reuse it has not been confirmed.

Questions and limits

The paper identifies therapeutic angiogenesis in living organisms as future work. The in-vitro results do not establish clinical efficacy.

How to compare the results

Compare cell number, spheroid size, culture and oxygen conditions, and the proportion of conditioned medium.
