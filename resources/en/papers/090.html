Ion‑Mediated Structural Engineering of Hydrogel Interfaces for Tunable Mechanical and Analyte Diffusion Properties in Electrochemical Biosensors

Dongwook Lee†, Soo A Kim†, Beom‐Jun Shim, Yurim Lee, Tae Young Kim, Sunghyun Park, Yeontaek Lee, Hyeong Gyu Choi, Kayoung Son, Su Bin Han, Keun-Young Yook, Seo Jung Kim, Won-Yong Lee, Jungmok Seo*, Jayoung Kim*

Advanced Materials, 38(19), e15767 (2026)

DOI: 10.1002/adma.202515767 ↗

Research context and placement

The gel interface jointly controls transport from biofluids to sensors and mechanical suitability, fitting tissue-coupling analyte transport.

Research overview and key contributions

Ion-treatment sequence tunes mechanics and analyte transport in PVA–alginate hydrogels.

Salting-out, coordination and biomineralization tailor structures for different sweat-sensing and insertable-interface requirements.

Open challenges

Editorial question: How stable are transport and calibration under biofluids, swelling and repeated loading?

Comparison context

Compare sensitivity, response time and strength at matched analyte, concentration, gel thickness and swelling state.

Related external research

Fully integrated wearable sensor arrays for multiplexed in situ perspiration analysis

DOI: 10.1038/nature16521 ↗

The comparator integrates multiplexed sweat analysis, temperature calibration and circuitry; #90 tunes the upstream transport/mechanical interface.

Different architectures, targets and calibration preclude sensitivity-only comparisons.
