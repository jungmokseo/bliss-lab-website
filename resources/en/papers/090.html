Ion‑Mediated Structural Engineering of Hydrogel Interfaces for Tunable Mechanical and Analyte Diffusion Properties in Electrochemical Biosensors

Dongwook Lee†, Soo A Kim†, Beom‐Jun Shim, Yurim Lee, Tae Young Kim, Sunghyun Park, Yeontaek Lee, Hyeong Gyu Choi, Kayoung Son, Su Bin Han, Keun-Young Yook, Seo Jung Kim, Won-Yong Lee, Jungmok Seo*, Jayoung Kim*

Advanced Materials, 38(19), e15767 (2026)

DOI: 10.1002/adma.202515767 ↗

Why this research matters

Changing the order of sulfate and calcium treatments tunes the mechanics and analyte transport of poly(vinyl alcohol)–alginate sensor interfaces. The study examines how the sequence of network and pore formation changes strength and diffusion pathways, even when the ions are the same.

The problem

A hydrogel can provide both a compliant sensor contact and a route for analytes to reach the electrode. Greater strength does not always mean faster transport. This study changes the order of ion treatments to tune the internal structure of PVA–alginate gels.

How the study approaches it

Sulfate treatment promotes PVA association and crystallization through salting-out; calcium forms ionic junctions in alginate. Reversing their order changes when junctions, pores and mineral structures form. The same ions can therefore produce different interfaces through a different processing history.

What was tested

Calcium-first treatment forms junctions before subsequent calcium-sulfate formation, giving a denser reinforced network. Sulfate-first treatment establishes porosity earlier. Structure, mechanics and transport measurements together explain how these architectures affect analyte access to a sensor.

What the study found

The study offers a way to select an interface for its measurement environment. Sweat sensing and insertable sensing place different demands on transport and mechanics. The structural explanation does not establish clinical accuracy for every sensor using the gel.

What remains to be answered

Comparisons need the same analyte, concentration, gel thickness and swelling state, with response time considered alongside strength. Biofluid interference and calibration during repeated loading require additional checks. A structural mechanism does not replace sensor-specific validation.

How it connects to other research

The related multiplexed sweat sensor focuses on temperature calibration and circuit integration. This study addresses the transport and mechanical interface before an analyte reaches the sensor. Different targets and architectures prevent a sensitivity ranking based on the external abstract.
