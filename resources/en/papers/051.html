Nonfluorinated Superomniphobic Surfaces through Shape-Tunable Mushroom-like Polymeric Micropillar Arrays

Hyunchul Kim*, Heetak Han, Sanggeun Lee, Janghoon Woo, Jungmok Seo*, Taeyoon Lee*

ACS Applied Materials & Interfaces, 11 (5), 5484-5491 (2019)

Jungmok Seo, who now leads the BLISS Lab in the School of Electrical and Electronic Engineering at Yonsei University, is the corresponding author of this paper.

DOI: 10.1021/acsami.8b17181 ↗

The question behind this study

Mushroom-shaped polymer micropillars use downward-curled edges to resist penetration by low-surface-tension liquids without fluorinated surface treatment. Aluminum deposition tunes the cap geometry, which is evaluated through wetting and deformation tests.

Repellency without fluorinated materials

This study uses nonfluorinated microgeometry to resist invasion by low-surface-tension liquids.

How curled mushroom caps support liquid

Aluminum (Al) deposition curls the caps of PDMS mushroom-shaped micropillars downward, supporting the liquid contact line.

Wetting and deformation tests

The main text and supplementary information evaluate wetting, deformation, plasma exposure and evaporation pressure. Separate videos are outside this account’s scope.

Stretching cycles and breakthrough pressure

The stretching plot reaches 1,000 cycles. Reported breakthrough pressure is approximately 85.7 Pa; rounded inputs yield 89.0 Pa, requiring clarification.

Blood repellency and biological validation

Horse-blood droplet repellency does not establish biological efficacy. Dedicated abrasion and biocompatibility assays were not identified.

Comparison with lubricated surfaces

The related SLIPS abstract provides a comparison of liquid-interface designs. It does not establish a performance ranking.
