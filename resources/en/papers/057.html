Antibacterial infection and immune-evasive coating for orthopedic implants

Kyomin Chae†, Woo Young Jang†, Kijun Park†, Jinhyeok Lee, Hyunchul Kim, Kyoungbun Lee, Chang Kyu Lee, Yeontaek Lee, Soon Hyuck Lee*, Jungmok Seo*

Science Advances, 6 (44), eabb0025 (2020)

DOI: 10.1126/sciadv.abb0025 ↗

Research context and placement

A lubricated interface on orthopedic fixation hardware is evaluated for bacterial attachment, infection-related inflammation and bone healing. Device-surface biological responses place it within host interaction.

Fouling resistance at lubricated interfaces

LOIS retains lubricant within micro/nanostructures to reduce fouling on orthopedic fixation hardware. Evaluation covered bacterial adhesion, surgical-handling damage and a rabbit fracture model.

Using air-dependent superhydrophobic surfaces as controls, the study examined lubricant-interface retention and recovery after damage, alongside infection-related inflammation and bone healing.

Open challenges

Precontaminated implants do not represent every infection route. The authors identify post-implantation infection models and selective osteoinductive-cell adhesion as follow-up questions.

Comparison context

Fair comparisons match organism, contamination timing, loading/damage, retained lubricant and bone-healing endpoints. Reduced surface adhesion must be distinguished from bacterial killing.
