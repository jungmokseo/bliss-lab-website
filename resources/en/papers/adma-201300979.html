Gas‐Driven Ultrafast Reversible Switching of Super‐hydrophobic Adhesion on Palladium‐Coated Silicon Nanowires

Jungmok Seo†, Soonil Lee†, Heetak Han, Hwae bong Jung, Juree Hong, Giyoung Song, Suk Man Cho, Cheolmin Park, Wooyoung Lee, Taeyoon Lee*

Advanced Materials, 25 (30), 4139-4144 (2013)

Jungmok Seo, who now leads the BLISS Lab in the School of Electrical and Electronic Engineering at Yonsei University, is a co-author of this paper.

DOI: 10.1002/adma.201300979 ↗

Background

Palladium-coated silicon nanowires change shape as they take up and release hydrogen. Switching the gas environment lets the surface hold or release water droplets without direct contact.

What the study found

Palladium-coated silicon nanowires change shape as they take up and release hydrogen. The study uses this change to switch droplet adhesion.

Changing the surrounding gas controls droplet pinning and release without direct contact with the surface.

Questions and limits

The DTS surface treatment and palladium thickness matter to operation. Biological analysis is a proposed application, rather than a demonstrated outcome.

How to compare the results

Compare hydrogen concentration, palladium thickness, droplet volume, surface tilt, and switching cycles.
