Gas‐Specific and Tunable Hydrogen‐Selective ZIF Membrane through Combined Physical Confinement and Sealing Techniques

Eunji Choi, Seungho Yu, Taehwan Kim, Yeontaek Lee, Hyungjoon Ji, Minsu Kim, Wooyoung Choi, Ju Yeon Kim, Yunseong Ji, Jungmok Seo, Kiwon Eum, Ki Chul Kim, Dae Woo Kim*

Small, 21(34), 2501590 (2025)

DOI: 10.1002/smll.202501590 ↗

Why this research matters

This gas-separation study physically confines and seals ZIF-8 membranes to control nonselective transport pathways. It examines how confinement, liquid sealing and surface fluorination separately affect hydrogen selectivity.

What the study did

The study combines GNR confinement and PFPE sealing to control nonselective paths through a ZIF-8 gas-separation membrane.

Confinement, sealing and surface fluorination play different roles in adjusting selectivity for different gas pairs.

What remains to be answered

Selected gas mixtures were tested at 25 °C and 1 bar. An open question is whether the balance between selectivity and permeance persists under humidity, changing pressure and prolonged operation.

What to compare

Separate pure-gas and mixed-gas results, and match the gas pair, pressure, temperature, membrane thickness and support.

Related research

A MOF Glass Membrane for Gas Separation

DOI: 10.1002/anie.201915807 ↗

The external abstract reports eliminating intercrystalline-defect formation by vitrifying ZIF-62. This study instead confines and seals a crystalline membrane.

Framework, phase, support and gas pairs differ, so separation factors alone cannot establish which approach is better.
