A facile method for the selective decoration of graphene defects based on a galvanic displacement reaction

Juree Hong, Jae-Bok Lee, Sanggeun Lee, Jungmok Seo, Hyunsoo Lee, Jeong Young Park, Jong-Hyun Ahn, Tae Il Seo, Taeyoon Lee*, Han-Bo-Ram Lee*

NPG Asia Materials, 8(4), e262 (2016)

DOI: 10.1038/am.2016.42 ↗

Background

Palladium nanoparticles selectively deposit at graphene defects, lowering sheet resistance and improving a transparent heater. This metal-deposition approach does not establish complete repair of the carbon lattice.

What the study found

Galvanic displacement selectively deposits palladium nanoparticles at defects in chemical-vapour-deposited graphene, improving sheet resistance and transparent-heater performance.

The method uses defect reactivity to direct deposition. Metal decoration does not establish complete restoration of the carbon lattice.

Questions and limits

The paper notes that decoration patterns alone make it difficult to distinguish grain boundaries from wrinkles.

How to compare the results

Compare palladium coverage, optical transmission, reaction time, substrate, and heater input power.

Related reading

Selective metal deposition at graphene line defects by atomic layer deposition

DOI: 10.1038/ncomms5781 ↗

The related paper’s abstract describes selective platinum deposition at graphene line defects by atomic layer deposition for hydrogen sensing. It offers a comparison with solution-based palladium decoration.

The metals, deposition processes and sensor or heater endpoints differ, so this is not a direct performance ranking.
