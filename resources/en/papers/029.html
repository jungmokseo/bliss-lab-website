Highly Stable Surface‐Enhanced Raman Spectroscopy Substrates Using Few‐Layer Graphene on Silver Nanoparticles

Jaehong Lee, Sera Shin, Subin Kang, Sanggeun Lee, Jungmok Seo, Taeyoon Lee*

Journal of Nanomaterials, 2015(1), 975043 (2015)

DOI: 10.1155/2015/975043 ↗

Background

A thin graphene coating protects silver nanoparticles used for surface-enhanced Raman spectroscopy. Oxidation tests and dye signals after heating in air examine both protection and retained sensing function.

What the study found

Few-layer graphene grown from a PMMA carbon source protects silver nanoparticles. The study evaluates oxidation and rhodamine 6G (R6G) Raman signals after annealing in air.

The solid carbon source limits particle aggregation during growth, while the thin coating preserves surface-enhanced Raman spectroscopy (SERS) activity.

Questions and limits

A question for further study is whether thermal oxidation resistance carries over to complex samples and long storage.

How to compare the results

Compare particle and coating geometry, annealing history, analyte concentration, and Raman optical conditions.

Related reading

Shell-isolated nanoparticle-enhanced Raman spectroscopy

DOI: 10.1038/nature08907 ↗

The related SHINERS paper’s abstract uses silica- or alumina-coated gold particles to limit aggregation and direct sample contact. It offers a comparison of protective coatings and optical access.

Differences in silver versus gold, shell chemistry and measurement geometry prevent simple comparisons of enhancement factors.
