Conductive Fiber‐Based Ultrasensitive Textile Pressure Sensor for Wearable Electronics

Jaehong Lee, Hyukho Kwon, Jungmok Seo, Sera Shin, Ja Hoon Koo, Changhyun Pang, Seungbae Son, Jae Hyung Kim, Yong Hoon Jang, Dae-Eun Kim, Taeyoon Lee*

Advanced Materials, 27 (15), 2433-2439 (2015)

Jungmok Seo, who now leads the BLISS Lab in the School of Electrical and Electronic Engineering at Yonsei University, is a co-author of this paper.

DOI: 10.1002/adma.201500009 ↗

Background

Conductive fibres coated with dielectric rubber form a pressure sensor that can be woven into textiles. The abstract describes glove and clothing demonstrations for wireless control.

What the study found

Conductive fibres coated with dielectric rubber form a flexible textile pressure sensor, demonstrated in woven gloves and clothing for wireless controls.

The fibres serve as pressure-sensing elements in a weavable structure. Assessing the abstract’s claims of superiority would require the detailed comparison conditions.

Questions and limits

A question for further study is how crossing-point friction, washing and tensile strain affect the pressure signal.

How to compare the results

Compare loading range, fibre-crossing geometry, dielectric thickness, readout circuit, and tensile strain.
