Bioadhesive hydrogels as immunomodulatory interfaces for chronic wound healing: from microenvironmental regulation mechanism to engineering strategies

Kayoung Son†, Soo A Kim†, Minkyong Kang, Yejin Jo, Chansoo Kim, Kijun Park, Yunmin Jung, Tae Young Kim, Jungmok Seo*

Burns & Trauma, tkag055 (2026)

DOI: 10.1093/burnst/tkag055 ↗

Research context and placement

Immunomodulation and the wound microenvironment are central; tissue adhesion provides a secondary connection.

Research overview and key contributions

The review frames bioadhesive hydrogels as immunomodulatory interfaces for chronic wounds.

It links adhesion, microenvironment and macrophage regulation across protective, delivery, externally activated and adaptive designs.

Open challenges

The authors identify adhesion under exudate/motion, long-term safety and manufacturing standardization as challenges.

Comparison context

Editorial comparison conditions: align wound model, exudate, adhesion test and immune-assessment timing; distinguish review synthesis from device outcomes.

Related external research

Wireless, closed-loop, smart bandage with integrated sensors and stimulators for advanced wound care and accelerated healing

DOI: 10.1038/s41587-022-01528-3 ↗

A mouse system integrating sensing, stimulation and detachable hydrogel exemplifies the review's adaptive-design category.

A specific closed-loop device and a heterogeneous hydrogel review cannot be directly ranked.
