Play‐Doh Inspired a Transformative, Shape‐Adaptive iHEÄLS Enabling Post‐Crosslinking Mechanical‐Biological Modulation and Its Effectiveness in Musculoskeletal Repair

Niyou Wang†, Chao Liang†, Soo A Kim†, Jessica Heline Lopes da Fonseca, María José Veana Hernandez, Zahra Rezaei, Alvaro Dario Martinez Blanco, Nicole Joy Bassous, Da Seul Kim, Yejin Jo, Tae Young Kim, Yipei Yang, Ziqi Huang, Siyuan Chen, Feiming Li, Marcos Akira dAvila, June-Seo Kim, Misun Kang, Jungmok Seo, Sang Jin Lee*, Su Ryon Shin*

Advanced Materials, e74385 (2026)

Jungmok Seo, who now leads the BLISS Lab in the School of Electrical and Electronic Engineering at Yonsei University, is a co-author of this paper.

DOI: 10.1002/adma.74385 ↗

Why this research matters

A triple-network hydrogel is designed to fill defects, adhere to tissue and recover its shape. The abstract describes post-crosslinking mechanical training to tune material and biological responses, with evaluation in a bone-regeneration model.

What the study did

The CNC-based triple-network iHEÄLS combines defect filling, tissue adhesion and shape recovery. The abstract reports evaluation in a bone-regeneration model.

Mechanical training after crosslinking is used to tune the network and its biological responses.

What remains to be answered

How training history and degradation affect sustained mechanical support and regeneration remains a question for closer study. Clinical efficacy has not been verified.

What to compare

Compare defect geometry, tissue and hydration, training direction and cycle count, autologous-bone mixing, and the timing of adhesion and regeneration assessments.
